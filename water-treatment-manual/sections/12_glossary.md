# Section 12 — Glossary

> Every term you need to know, in plain English. When you see a word you don't recognize in this manual or from your supervisor, look here first.

---

| Term | Plain-English Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Adsorption** | A process where dissolved molecules stick to the surface of a solid material (like activated carbon). Different from absorption — adsorption is surface sticking, not soaking in. |
| **Alkalinity** | A measure of water's ability to resist changes in pH (its "buffering capacity"). Expressed as mg/L as CaCO₃. Low alkalinity means pH can swing dramatically when you add a coagulant. |
| **Alum** | Common name for aluminum sulphate — the most widely used coagulant in water treatment. A white crystalline solid or clear liquid solution. |
| **Backwash** | Reversing the flow through a filter (or sending water upward through the bed) to clean out trapped particles and restore filter capacity. |
| **Backwash expansion** | How much the filter bed "fluffs up" during backwashing. Typically targeted at 20–30% above the settled bed depth. Too little and the bed doesn't clean; too much and media washes out. |
| **Biofilm** | A community of bacteria that attaches to pipe walls or equipment surfaces and forms a slimy layer. Biofilm in distribution pipes can shelter pathogens from disinfectants and consume chlorine residual. |
| **BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand)** | A measure of how much oxygen microorganisms will consume to break down organic matter in a water sample. High BOD = high organic content. |
| **Boil Water Advisory (BWA)** | An official public notice issued when drinking water may be microbiologically unsafe. Issued by the regulatory authority, not the operator. |
| **Buffer / Buffering capacity** | A solution's ability to resist pH changes. Water with high alkalinity is well-buffered — adding acid or base doesn't change pH much. Low alkalinity = poorly buffered = pH swings easily. |
| **Cavitation** | A damaging phenomenon in pumps: low-pressure zones cause water to vaporize into tiny bubbles, which then collapse violently. Sounds like gravel in the pump. Erodes impeller metal over time. |
| **Chloramine** | A compound formed when chlorine reacts with ammonia. A weaker disinfectant than free chlorine but longer-lasting residual in distribution. Some utilities add ammonia intentionally ("chloramination"). |
| **Chlorine demand** | The amount of chlorine consumed by organic matter, iron, manganese, and other substances in water before any residual is established. Demand must be satisfied before a residual can exist. |
| **Chlorine residual** | The amount of chlorine remaining in treated water after the demand has been met. "Free chlorine residual" = hypochlorous acid + hypochlorite ion. |
| **Clarifier** | A tank or basin that uses settling (and sometimes chemical treatment) to remove suspended solids from water. Same basic idea as a sedimentation basin. |
| **Coagulant** | A chemical (typically alum or PACl) that neutralizes the electrical charge on suspended particles, allowing them to clump together. |
| **Coagulation** | The process of neutralizing particle charges so they can stick together. The first step before flocculation. |
| **Coliform bacteria** | A group of bacteria used as indicators of faecal contamination. Total coliform = broad group (some are non-faecal). E. coli = specifically faecal — a positive E. coli result is a serious concern. |
| **Combined chlorine** | Chlorine that has reacted with ammonia to form chloramines. Total chlorine − free chlorine = combined chlorine. |
| **Confined space** | An enclosed or partially enclosed space not designed for continuous occupancy, with limited entry/exit, that may have an atmospheric hazard. Examples: clear well, wet well, buried valve vault. |
| **Contact time (CT)** | Concentration of disinfectant (mg/L) × time (minutes) = CT value. Regulators specify minimum CT to achieve pathogen inactivation. Higher CT = better disinfection. |
| **Cryptosporidium** | A protozoan parasite resistant to chlorine at practical doses. Causes cryptosporidiosis (severe diarrhea). Removed by filtration and inactivated by UV. |
| **DBPs (Disinfection Byproducts)** | Compounds formed when chlorine reacts with natural organic matter (NOM). Primary DBPs of concern: trihalomethanes (THMs) and haloacetic acids (HAAs). Some are probable carcinogens. |
| **Differential pressure (DP)** | The difference in pressure between two points — across a filter, valve, or meter. As a filter clogs, its DP increases. Used as the primary indicator of when to backwash. |
| **DPD** | N,N-Diethyl-p-phenylenediamine — the reagent used in the colorimetric test for free and total chlorine. DPD-1 = free chlorine; DPD-3 = total chlorine. |
| **E. coli (Escherichia coli)** | A faecal coliform bacterium used as the primary indicator of sewage or faecal contamination. Any detectable E. coli in finished water is unacceptable and triggers mandatory reporting. |
| **Effluent** | Water leaving a treatment process. "Filter effluent" = water that has passed through the filter. |
| **Floc** | The clumps of particles formed during flocculation. Healthy floc looks like white or grey snowflakes. Good floc settles quickly and is easy to filter. |
| **Flocculation** | The process of gently mixing coagulated water to cause particles to collide and stick together into larger, heavier floc. Follows coagulation. |
| **Flow meter** | An instrument that measures the volume of water passing a point per unit time (L/min, m³/hour, MGD). Essential for controlling chemical doses. |
| **Free chlorine** | Chlorine in its active disinfecting forms: hypochlorous acid (HOCl) and hypochlorite ion (OCl⁻). HOCl is 80× more effective as a disinfectant than OCl⁻. |
| **G-value** | A measure of mixing intensity (turbulence) in units of s⁻¹. Rapid mix uses high G (300–1000 s⁻¹); flocculation uses low G (10–75 s⁻¹). |
| **GHS (Globally Harmonized System)** | The international standard for chemical hazard communication — standardizes hazard labels and SDS formats worldwide. |
| **Giardia** | A protozoan parasite. More resistant to chlorine than bacteria but less so than Cryptosporidium. Removed by filtration; inactivated by UV or sufficient CT. Causes giardiasis ("beaver fever"). |
| **HAA (Haloacetic Acids)** | A class of chlorination disinfection byproducts (DBPs). Five HAAs (HAA5) are regulated. Formed when chlorine reacts with NOM. |
| **Head / Headloss** | "Head" is a way of expressing pressure using the height of a water column. "Headloss" is the pressure drop across a filter or pipe — caused by resistance. Measured in metres or feet. |
| **HOCl (Hypochlorous acid)** | The most effective form of free chlorine. Forms at lower pH. At pH 7.5, roughly 50% of free chlorine is HOCl; at pH 6.5, ~90% is HOCl. This is why pH control matters for disinfection. |
| **Hydraulic detention time (HDT)** | How long water spends in a basin or chamber. HDT = Volume ÷ Flow rate. Longer HDT = more settling or more disinfection contact time. |
| **Hypochlorite (NaOCl)** | The weaker ionic form of free chlorine (OCl⁻). Less effective as a disinfectant than HOCl but more prevalent at higher pH. Also the name for liquid bleach. |
| **Influent** | Water entering a treatment process. "Filter influent" = water about to enter the filter. Opposite of effluent. |
| **Jar test** | A small-scale laboratory simulation of coagulation/flocculation to determine the optimal chemical dose before making changes to the full-scale process. |
| **kW / kWh** | Kilowatt (power) and kilowatt-hour (energy). Track these for operational efficiency and cost management. Large pumps are the biggest energy consumers. |
| **Lamella settler / tube settler / plate settler** | Inclined plates or tubes inside a clarifier that dramatically increase the effective settling area without increasing the basin footprint. |
| **LOTO (Lockout/Tagout)** | The procedure for isolating equipment from all energy sources before maintenance. Prevents workers from being injured by unexpected startup. |
| **MCL (Maximum Contaminant Level)** | The legal maximum concentration of a contaminant in drinking water. Set by provincial regulations. |
| **Membrane filtration** | Filtration through semi-permeable polymer membranes with very small pores. Types: microfiltration (MF), ultrafiltration (UF), nanofiltration (NF), reverse osmosis (RO). |
| **mg/L** | Milligrams per litre. The standard unit for expressing chemical concentrations in water. 1 mg/L ≈ 1 ppm (part per million). |
| **µg/L** | Micrograms per litre. Used for trace contaminants. 1 µg/L = 0.001 mg/L = 1 ppb (part per billion). |
| **NOM (Natural Organic Matter)** | Dissolved organic material from decomposing vegetation, algae, and soil. Causes colour and taste in water. Reacts with chlorine to form DBPs. |
| **NTU (Nephelometric Turbidity Unit)** | The unit of turbidity measurement. Named after the nephelometric measurement method (measuring scattered light). |
| **Operating permit** | The legally binding document from your regulatory authority that specifies exactly how your plant must operate. |
| **PACl (Polyaluminium Chloride)** | A pre-polymerized aluminum coagulant. Works better than alum at cold temperatures and over a wider pH range. |
| **PAC (Powdered Activated Carbon)** | A fine carbon powder used to adsorb taste and odour compounds (geosmin, MIB) during algal events. Added upstream of coagulation. |
| **Pathogen** | A disease-causing microorganism. In water treatment, primary concerns are bacteria (E. coli, Salmonella), viruses (norovirus, hepatitis A), and protozoa (Giardia, Cryptosporidium). |
| **pH** | A scale (0–14) measuring how acidic or basic a solution is. pH 7 = neutral; < 7 = acidic; > 7 = basic. Critical for coagulation effectiveness and chlorine disinfection. |
| **P&ID (Piping and Instrumentation Diagram)** | A technical drawing showing all pipes, equipment, valves, and instruments in a process and how they interconnect. Your roadmap to the plant. |
| **PPE (Personal Protective Equipment)** | Equipment worn to protect against workplace hazards. Includes gloves, goggles, face shields, respirators, hard hats, etc. |
| **Rapid mix** | The high-speed, short-duration mixing step immediately after coagulant addition. Disperses the coagulant uniformly before it reacts unevenly. |
| **Residual** | What's left after demand has been satisfied. "Chlorine residual" = chlorine remaining after demand is met. "Residual pressure" = pressure remaining at the end of a distribution pipe. |
| **SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition)** | A computer-based control and monitoring system. Shows real-time readings from instruments, logs data, and allows remote control of equipment. |
| **SDS (Safety Data Sheet)** | A document provided by chemical manufacturers that contains hazard information, handling instructions, first aid, spill response, and disposal guidance. Required for every chemical on-site. |
| **Sedimentation** | The removal of suspended particles from water by gravity settling. Particles denser than water sink to the bottom. |
| **Short-circuiting** | When water flows through a basin or contact chamber faster than the theoretical HDT — bypassing the full treatment time. Caused by uneven flow, wind, temperature stratification, or poor baffling. |
| **Sludge** | Settled particles removed from the bottom of sedimentation basins and clarifiers. Must be removed periodically to prevent it from re-suspending and carrying over to the filters. |
| **Softening** | Removal of hardness (calcium and magnesium) from water. Not always required; depends on source water and customer needs. |
| **SOR (Surface Overflow Rate)** | Flow rate ÷ basin surface area. A key design parameter for sedimentation. If SOR is too high, particles don't have time to settle before they overflow. |
| **SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)** | A written, step-by-step procedure for performing a task. This manual is a collection of SOPs. |
| **Surface water** | Water from a lake, river, stream, or reservoir. Subject to runoff and seasonal variation. Requires more treatment than groundwater in most cases. |
| **THM (Trihalomethane)** | A class of chlorination disinfection byproducts. The four most common (TTHM) are chloroform, bromodichloromethane, dibromochloromethane, and bromoform. Probable human carcinogens at high exposure. |
| **Turbidity** | A measure of water cloudiness, caused by suspended particles scattering light. Measured in NTU. High turbidity in filtered water = inadequate treatment. Turbidity can "shield" pathogens from disinfectants. |
| **UV (Ultraviolet)** | Electromagnetic radiation used to disinfect water by damaging pathogen DNA. Effective against Cryptosporidium (which chlorine cannot adequately inactivate at practical doses). |
| **UVTOC** | See NOM. |
| **UVT (UV Transmittance)** | The percentage of UV light that passes through a water sample at 254 nm wavelength. Higher UVT = more UV gets to pathogens = more effective disinfection. Turbidity and NOM reduce UVT. |
| **Water hammer** | A pressure surge in a pipe caused by a sudden change in flow velocity — e.g., rapidly closing a valve or starting/stopping a pump abruptly. Can crack pipes and damage valves. |
| **WHMIS (Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System)** | Canada's hazard communication standard, now aligned with GHS. Covers labels and SDS requirements. |
| **Zeta potential** | The electrical charge on suspended particles in water. Most particles are negatively charged, which keeps them apart (they repel each other). Coagulants neutralize this charge, allowing particles to approach and stick. |
