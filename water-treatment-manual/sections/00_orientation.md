# Section 0 — Plant Orientation

> **Before you touch anything:** read this section completely. Understanding the big picture makes every other procedure make sense.

---

## 0.1 What a Water Treatment Plant Actually Does

Water from a source (river, lake, well, or reservoir) is not safe to drink straight. It contains:

| What's in it | Why it's a problem |
|---|---|
| Suspended solids (dirt, silt, algae) | Clouds the water, carries pathogens |
| Bacteria, viruses, protozoa | Cause disease (E. coli, Giardia, Cryptosporidium) |
| Dissolved chemicals (iron, manganese, nitrates) | Toxic at certain levels, cause taste/odour issues |
| Natural organic matter (NOM) | Reacts with disinfectants to form harmful byproducts |

The plant takes that raw water through a series of treatment steps and produces water that meets regulatory standards — safe enough to drink, bathe in, and cook with.

---

## 0.2 The Treatment Train (Overview)

Think of it as an assembly line. Water moves forward through each step:

```
RAW WATER INTAKE
      │
      ▼
[1] PRE-TREATMENT
    (screening, pre-chlorination if used)
      │
      ▼
[2] COAGULATION / FLOCCULATION
    (chemicals added to clump particles together)
      │
      ▼
[3] SEDIMENTATION / CLARIFICATION
    (heavy clumps sink and are removed)
      │
      ▼
[4] FILTRATION
    (remaining particles caught in sand/anthracite/membrane)
      │
      ▼
[5] DISINFECTION
    (chlorine/UV/ozone kills pathogens)
      │
      ▼
[6] pH ADJUSTMENT / STABILIZATION
    (protect pipes from corrosion)
      │
      ▼
[7] CLEAR WELL / STORAGE
    (treated water held before distribution)
      │
      ▼
DISTRIBUTION SYSTEM (your customers)
```

> **Your job:** Keep each step running correctly so the water leaving the plant is always safe. If one step fails, the ones after it work harder — and sometimes can't compensate. That's why you monitor everything.
