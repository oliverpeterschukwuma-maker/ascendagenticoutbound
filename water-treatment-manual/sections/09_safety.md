# Section 9 — Safety & PPE

> Water treatment plants have real hazards. People are seriously injured and killed in this industry every year — almost always because a shortcut was taken or a procedure was skipped. Safety procedures are not bureaucratic obstacles. They are the distillation of hard lessons learned by people who got hurt.

---

## 9.1 The Non-Negotiable Safety Rules

1. **Never bypass LOTO (Lockout/Tagout).** Energized equipment kills.
2. **Never enter a confined space alone or without a permit.**
3. **Always wear the required PPE.** If you don't know what's required, don't start the task.
4. **If you're unsure, stop and ask.** The question you feel embarrassed to ask is usually the most important one.
5. **Report every near-miss.** Near-misses are accidents that didn't quite happen — they predict future accidents.

---

## 9.2 Site Hazard Overview

| Hazard Category | Where You'll Find It | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical burns / splash | Chemical storage rooms, dosing areas, any injection point | Skin burns, eye damage, lung damage |
| Toxic gas | Chlorine room, confined spaces (H₂S, CO₂, low O₂) | Unconsciousness, death |
| Drowning | Open basins, wet wells, clear well | Death |
| Electrocution | MCC rooms, panels, pump controls, exposed wiring | Death, severe burns |
| Crushing / entanglement | Pumps, mixers, scrapers, conveyors with moving parts | Loss of limb, death |
| Slips, trips, falls | Wet floors near pumps and basins, ladders, grates | Fractures, concussions |
| Manual handling | Chemical drums, bags of lime, equipment | Back injuries, hernias |

## 9.3 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Guide

### When to wear what

| Task | Required PPE |
|---|---|
| General plant rounds | Safety glasses, hard-toed boots, hard hat in construction zones |
| Working near open water (basins) | Life jacket if working within 1m of open water; buddy system |
| Handling liquid chemicals (alum, PACl, NaOCl, fluoride) | Chemical splash goggles (not just safety glasses), chemical-resistant gloves (nitrile minimum; neoprene for acids), apron |
| Handling concentrated acids (fluorosilicic acid, H₂SO₄) | Face shield + chemical splash goggles, acid-resistant gloves (thick), acid apron, boots with no metal |
| Handling dry lime or powdered chemicals | N95 dust mask, goggles, gloves |
| Electrical work | Electrical insulating gloves (appropriate voltage rating), arc flash PPE per electrical risk assessment |
| Entering confined space | Follow full confined space entry procedure (Section 9.6) |
| Chlorine room / gas chlorine systems | SCBA (Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus), chemical suit — trained responders only |
| Noise > 85 dB (large pump rooms) | Hearing protection (earplugs or earmuffs) |
| Working at height > 1.2 m (ladders, catwalks) | Fall arrest harness attached to anchor point |

### How to put on (don) and remove (doff) chemical PPE

**Donning (putting on — clean hands first):**
1. Apron
2. Gloves (check for holes by inflating)
3. Goggles / face shield

**Doffing (removing — assume contaminated):**
1. Gloves (peel off inside-out so contaminated surface faces in)
2. Goggles (handle by the strap)
3. Apron (roll away from body)
4. Wash hands immediately

## 9.4 WHMIS / GHS — Understanding Hazard Labels

Every chemical container in a water plant must have a GHS-compliant label. Know these symbols:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Flame | Flammable |
| Skull and crossbones | Acutely toxic (can kill) |
| Exclamation mark | Irritant, less severe toxicity |
| Corrosion | Destroys skin and metal |
| Gas cylinder | Compressed gas |
| Exploding bomb | Explosive |
| Flame over circle | Oxidizer (increases fire risk) |
| Health hazard (person with starburst) | Carcinogen, reproductive hazard, organ damage |
| Environment | Environmental hazard |

**Safety Data Sheets (SDS):**
Every chemical must have an SDS on file and accessible at all times. The SDS tells you:
- What the chemical is and its hazards
- First aid measures
- Firefighting measures
- Spill response procedures
- Handling and storage requirements
- Exposure limits

`[ACTION]` Locate the SDS binder or station for your site. Know exactly where it is — you may need it in an emergency.

## 9.5 Lockout / Tagout (LOTO) Procedure

> Also described in Section 7.3 (Maintenance). Repeated here because it's critical enough to deserve two mentions.

**The 6 steps:**

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| **1. Prepare** | Identify all energy sources (electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic, gravity, stored pressure, chemical) |
| **2. Notify** | Tell all affected workers that equipment is being locked out |
| **3. Shut down** | Turn off the equipment through normal stopping procedure |
| **4. Isolate** | Open all disconnects, close all isolation valves, bleed stored pressure |
| **5. Lock and tag** | Place YOUR lock on each isolation point. Attach a tag with your name and the date |
| **6. Verify** | Try to start the equipment (it should not start). Check all pressure gauges read zero. |

**When LOTO is removed:**
1. Confirm all workers are clear of the equipment.
2. Remove all tools, materials, and covers.
3. Each person removes ONLY their own lock.
4. Notify all affected workers.
5. Start up equipment and verify normal operation.

## 9.6 Confined Space Entry

Confined spaces at a water plant include: clear wells, sedimentation basins, filter underfloor access, wet wells, chemical storage vaults, and any other enclosed space with limited entry/exit not designed for continuous occupancy.

**Hazards specific to confined spaces:**
- Oxygen deficiency (O₂ displaced by CO₂, N₂, or methane)
- Toxic gases (H₂S from biological decomposition, chlorine)
- Flooding
- Engulfment (sludge or water)

**Permit-required confined space entry procedure:**

`⚠️ WARNING:` Do NOT enter a confined space without a written entry permit and trained attendant. This is non-negotiable.

1. **Hazard assessment:** Identify all hazards before opening the space.
2. **Atmospheric testing:** Test for O₂ (must be 19.5–23.5%), flammable gases (< 10% LEL), and toxic gases (H₂S < 1 ppm, CO < 25 ppm for entry without supplied air) using a calibrated gas monitor. Test before entry AND continuously during entry.
3. **Ventilation:** Force-ventilate the space for at least 5 minutes before entry; continue ventilating during entry.
4. **Entry permit:** Complete and sign the entry permit. It must list: hazards, control measures, rescue plan, PPE required, atmospheric test results.
5. **Attendant:** A trained, equipped attendant must remain at the entry point at all times. They do not enter the space. Their job is to monitor and summon rescue if needed.
6. **Rescue plan:** Know exactly how you will rescue an incapacitated entrant (tripod and lifeline, or other). Never do a non-entry rescue attempt (no one has ever "quickly jumped in to save someone" in a confined space and had it go well — you become the second victim).
7. **Communication:** Maintain continuous contact between entrant and attendant.

## 9.7 Electrical Safety

- Only qualified electricians work on live circuits.
- Operators should know the location of all main disconnects and emergency shutoffs.
- **Arc flash PPE:** High-voltage panels have arc flash hazard labels. Do not open these panels without the correct arc flash PPE (based on the incident energy level on the label).
- Never reset a tripped breaker more than twice without investigating the cause.
- Never use water near electrical equipment without de-energizing first.
- Report any unusual heat, smell, discolouration, or sparking from electrical equipment immediately.

## 9.8 Chemical Safety — First Aid Summary

> Always consult the specific SDS for the chemical involved. These are general first aid guidelines.

| Exposure Type | Immediate Action |
|---|---|
| Skin contact with corrosive chemical | Immediately flush with large amounts of water for at least 20 minutes. Remove contaminated clothing while flushing. Seek medical attention. |
| Eye contact with any chemical | Immediately flush at the eyewash station for at least 15 minutes. Hold eyelids open. Seek medical attention even if no pain. |
| Inhalation of chlorine gas | Move to fresh air immediately. If breathing is affected, call 911. Do NOT re-enter the contaminated area without SCBA. |
| Ingestion of any chemical | Do NOT induce vomiting unless SDS specifically directs it. Call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222 in the US; check your regional number). Seek medical attention. |

**Eyewash station use:**
1. Pull the activation handle (this starts the water flow).
2. Hold eyelids open with fingers (your instinct is to squeeze them shut — override it).
3. Roll eyes in all directions.
4. Flush for a full 15 minutes — set a timer; it feels much longer than it is.
5. Call for help — don't flush alone if you can avoid it.
