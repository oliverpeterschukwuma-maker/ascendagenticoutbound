# Section 3 — Shutdown Procedures

> Shutdown is the reverse of startup — but with some important differences. The goal is to leave the plant in a safe, stable, known state that can be restarted cleanly.

---

## 3.1 Types of Shutdown

| Type | Cause | Urgency | Procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Planned shutdown** | Scheduled maintenance, holiday, seasonal | Low — time to prepare | Section 3.2 |
| **Controlled emergency shutdown** | Equipment failure, power issue, contamination event | Medium — minutes, not hours | Section 3.3 |
| **Emergency stop (E-stop)** | Imminent safety hazard to people | Immediate | Section 3.4 |

---

## 3.2 Planned Shutdown Procedure

### Preparation (day before or hours before)

- [ ] Notify your supervisor and any downstream users if water supply will be interrupted
- [ ] Fill the clear well / storage reservoir to maximum capacity — this buffers supply during the shutdown
- [ ] Confirm chemical inventory is sufficient for restart (no point fixing equipment and having no alum)
- [ ] Document the current state of all processes (flow rates, doses, pressures) — this is your baseline to return to

### Shutdown Sequence

> Work in reverse order from the distribution system back to intake. This ensures you are never pushing untreated water forward.

`[ACTION]` 1. Reduce raw water flow gradually over 15–30 minutes (don't shut off suddenly — water hammer).  
`[ACTION]` 2. Reduce chemical doses proportionally as flow decreases.  
`[ACTION]` 3. Stop the raw water pump(s). Close the intake valve.  
`[ACTION]` 4. Stop coagulant and any pre-treatment chemical dosing pumps.  
`[ACTION]` 5. Allow flocculation and sedimentation basins to drain to waste or remain filled (per your SOP — keeping basins full prevents structural damage from differential pressure on walls).  
`[ACTION]` 6. Close filter inlet valves. Note filter head levels.  
`[ACTION]` 7. Reduce chlorine dosing to zero OR maintain a minimal dose if water will sit in contact chamber for an extended period.  
`[ACTION]` 8. Close the entry point valve to the distribution system (only when clear well level is sufficient for the shutdown period).  
`[ACTION]` 9. Record shutdown time, reason, and plant state in the logbook.  
`[ACTION]` 10. Apply LOTO to all equipment that maintenance will work on (see Section 9).

### After Shutdown

- [ ] Walk the plant — confirm all equipment is stopped that should be stopped
- [ ] Secure chemical rooms (locked, ventilated)
- [ ] Confirm clear well level is logged
- [ ] Post a "PLANT SHUTDOWN — DO NOT OPERATE" notice on the main panel if you will be away

## 3.3 Controlled Emergency Shutdown

These situations require faster action but still allow a methodical sequence:

**Triggers:** Major equipment failure (pump cavitation, motor failure), chemical feed system failure, pipe break, power fluctuation.

`[ACTION]` 1. Immediately assess: Is the problem affecting finished water quality?
  - If YES: Close the distribution entry valve NOW, then troubleshoot.
  - If NO: You may have time for an orderly shutdown.

`[ACTION]` 2. Stop the raw water pump(s).  
`[ACTION]` 3. Stop chemical dosing.  
`[ACTION]` 4. Record the time, the triggering event, and all current readings in the logbook.  
`[ACTION]` 5. Notify your supervisor immediately.  
`[ACTION]` 6. Follow emergency response procedures if the event involves a chemical spill, contamination, or safety hazard (Section 10).

---

## 3.4 Emergency Stop (E-Stop)

Use only when there is an immediate threat to human safety (fire near chemical storage, someone in the water, gas leak, electrical arc).

`[ACTION]` Press the E-stop button on the main panel (red, mushroom-head button).

This cuts power to all process equipment simultaneously.

`⚠️ WARNING:` E-stop does NOT close valves — water may continue to flow by gravity. After pressing E-stop, manually close the intake and distribution valves if safe to do so.

`[ACTION]` After the E-stop: call emergency services if needed, then notify your supervisor. Do NOT restart the plant after an E-stop without supervisor authorization and a full inspection.

---

## 3.5 Extended Shutdown (More Than 24 Hours)

If the plant will be offline for more than a day:

- [ ] Drain and flush chemical dosing lines to prevent crystallization (especially alum and phosphoric acid lines)
- [ ] Leave filter media wet (drying out damages the bed structure and biological layer)
- [ ] Maintain at least a minimal chlorine residual in the clear well to prevent bacterial regrowth
- [ ] Set up a monitoring schedule — someone should check the plant daily even during extended shutdowns
- [ ] If temperatures will drop below freezing: drain any lines or instruments that could freeze and crack

---

## 3.6 Post-Shutdown Checklist

Before leaving the plant after any shutdown:

- [ ] Logbook entry complete (time, reason, final readings, who was notified)
- [ ] All relevant LOTO applied and documented
- [ ] Chemical rooms secured
- [ ] All exterior doors locked
- [ ] Supervisors and relevant contacts notified
- [ ] Clear well level recorded
- [ ] Any active alarms acknowledged and documented
