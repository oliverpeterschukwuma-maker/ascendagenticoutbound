## Fundamentals (Module 1)

**1. FRONT:** What is influent?

> **BACK:** Water flowing INTO the plant or a process. Operators watch its flow, colour, odour and strength — the first warning of trouble.

**2. FRONT:** What is effluent?

> **BACK:** Water flowing OUT of the plant or a process. Final effluent must meet permit limits.

**3. FRONT:** What is BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand)?

> **BACK:** Biochemical Oxygen Demand — oxygen used by bacteria to break down organics in 5 days at 20 °C. Measures wastewater 'strength'; used for loading, F/M (Food-to-Microorganism ratio) and permit compliance.

**4. FRONT:** What is CBOD (Carbonaceous Biochemical Oxygen Demand)?

> **BACK:** Carbonaceous BOD — BOD with nitrification inhibited, so only carbon-based demand is measured. Many permits use CBOD.

**5. FRONT:** What is COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand)?

> **BACK:** Chemical Oxygen Demand — oxygen equivalent of organics oxidized by a strong chemical (dichromate) in ~2 h. Fast; always ≥ BOD.

**6. FRONT:** What is TSS (Total Suspended Solids)?

> **BACK:** Total Suspended Solids — solids caught on a glass-fibre filter, dried at 103–105 °C. Shows solids removal and effluent quality.

**7. FRONT:** What is TDS (Total Dissolved Solids)?

> **BACK:** Total Dissolved Solids — solids that pass through the filter (salts, minerals). Not removed by clarifiers.

**8. FRONT:** TS = ?

> **BACK:** TS = TSS + TDS.

**9. FRONT:** Volatile vs fixed solids?

> **BACK:** Volatile = burns off at 550 °C = organic. Fixed = ash left = inorganic.

**10. FRONT:** What is DO (Dissolved Oxygen)?

> **BACK:** Dissolved Oxygen (mg/L). Aerobic bugs and fish need it. ~9 mg/L (milligrams per litre) saturation at 20 °C; warm water holds less.

**11. FRONT:** Fresh vs septic wastewater?

> **BACK:** Fresh: grey, musty. Septic: black, rotten-egg (H₂S) odour — safety and treatment problem.

**12. FRONT:** What is pH and why care?

> **BACK:** Acidity scale 0–14 (7 neutral; each unit = 10×). Bugs like ~6.5–8.5; sudden swings may mean industrial discharge.

**13. FRONT:** What is alkalinity?

> **BACK:** Buffering capacity ('pH shock absorber'), mg/L as CaCO₃ (calcium carbonate). Nitrification and chemical P removal use it up.

**14. FRONT:** Typical raw domestic BOD and TSS?

> **BACK:** About 200 mg/L each (medium strength). COD about twice BOD.

**15. FRONT:** What is a pathogen?

> **BACK:** A disease-causing organism: bacteria, viruses, protozoa (Giardia, Cryptosporidium), helminths.

**16. FRONT:** Why test for E. coli / fecal coliform?

> **BACK:** They are indicator organisms — their presence shows fecal contamination and possible pathogens. Used to check disinfection.

**17. FRONT:** Nutrient ratio for biology?

> **BACK:** BOD : N : P ≈ 100 : 5 : 1.

**18. FRONT:** What is eutrophication?

> **BACK:** Excess nutrients (especially P) → algae blooms → oxygen depletion when algae die → fish kills.

**19. FRONT:** Infiltration vs inflow?

> **BACK:** Infiltration = groundwater leaking in through cracks. Inflow = direct connections (roof drains, manhole lids).

**20. FRONT:** Effect of temperature on biology and DO?

> **BACK:** Warmer → faster biology but LESS DO saturation. Colder → slower biology (nitrification!), more DO.

## Safety (Module 2)

**21. FRONT:** Hierarchy of controls (best → worst)?

> **BACK:** Elimination → Substitution → Engineering → Administrative → PPE (last line of defence).

**22. FRONT:** H₂S (hydrogen sulphide) key facts?

> **BACK:** Rotten eggs; heavier than air (SG (Specific Gravity) ~1.19) — collects low; toxic; flammable; deadens smell (olfactory fatigue ~100 ppm (parts per million)).

**23. FRONT:** Methane key facts?

> **BACK:** Odourless; lighter than air (SG ~0.55) — collects high; explosive 5–15% in air.

**24. FRONT:** Chlorine gas key facts?

> **BACK:** Greenish-yellow; ~2.5× heavier than air; toxic/corrosive; supports combustion; liquid expands ~460× to gas.

**25. FRONT:** Acceptable oxygen range for entry?

> **BACK:** 19.5% to 23%. Normal air = 20.9%. Below 19.5% = deficient.

**26. FRONT:** Flammable gas limit for entry?

> **BACK:** Below 10% of the LEL (Lower Explosive Limit). 10% LEL methane = 0.5% methane by volume.

**27. FRONT:** Gas testing order?

> **BACK:** Oxygen → Flammables (LEL) → Toxics. Test top, middle and bottom. ('Only Fools Trust')

**28. FRONT:** Confined space?

> **BACK:** Enclosed/partly enclosed, not designed for continuous occupancy, restricted entry/exit. E.g., wet wells, digesters, manholes.

**29. FRONT:** Attendant's role?

> **BACK:** Stays OUTSIDE, monitors entrant, summons help, performs non-entry rescue. NEVER enters.

**30. FRONT:** Lockout key steps?

> **BACK:** Notify → shut down → isolate → each worker applies own lock → release stored energy → VERIFY (try to start) → work → remove own lock.

**31. FRONT:** Is a stop button or HOA (Hand-Off-Auto, switch) 'Off' lockout?

> **BACK:** No. Control devices are not energy isolation. Lock the disconnect and verify.

**32. FRONT:** How to find a small chlorine leak?

> **BACK:** Hold ammonia vapour near fittings — a white cloud shows the leak. Never put water on a leak.

**33. FRONT:** Eyewash/shower flush time?

> **BACK:** At least 15 minutes (longer for caustics per SDS (Safety Data Sheet)), then medical attention. Test stations weekly.

**34. FRONT:** SDS key sections?

> **BACK:** 2 hazards, 4 first aid, 6 spills, 7 storage, 8 PPE/exposure, 10 reactivity. 16 sections total.

**35. FRONT:** Never mix hypochlorite with…?

> **BACK:** Acids (→ chlorine gas) or ammonia (→ chloramine gases).

**36. FRONT:** Calcium hypochlorite storage hazard?

> **BACK:** Strong oxidizer — fire/explosion with oil, grease, rags or other organics. Keep dry and separate.

**37. FRONT:** Mixing acid and water?

> **BACK:** Always add Acid to water (AAA).

**38. FRONT:** Can a cartridge respirator be used in a wet well?

> **BACK:** Not if O₂ is low or contaminants are unknown/IDLH — air-purifying respirators don't supply oxygen. Use SCBA/supplied air.

**39. FRONT:** Fire extinguisher classes?

> **BACK:** A = ordinary combustibles (ash); B = flammable liquids (barrel); C = energized electrical (current — no water); D = metals; K = kitchen.

**40. FRONT:** Ladder rule?

> **BACK:** 4:1 angle; extend ~1 m above landing; 3 points of contact.

**41. FRONT:** BC worker rights?

> **BACK:** Right to know, right to participate, right to refuse unsafe work (report to supervisor).

**42. FRONT:** Main route of biological infection for operators?

> **BACK:** Ingestion (hand-to-mouth). Wash hands before eating, drinking or smoking.

## Preliminary treatment (Module 3)

**43. FRONT:** Purpose of preliminary treatment?

> **BACK:** Protect downstream equipment by removing rags, debris and grit. Removes little BOD.

**44. FRONT:** Screen vs comminutor?

> **BACK:** Screen REMOVES material. Comminutor SHREDS it and leaves it in the flow (rags can re-form mats downstream).

**45. FRONT:** What starts a mechanical bar screen rake?

> **BACK:** A timer and/or differential level (headloss) across the screen; high level override.

**46. FRONT:** Blinded screen symptom?

> **BACK:** Upstream water level rises; headloss/differential increases.

**47. FRONT:** What is grit?

> **BACK:** Heavy inorganic particles (sand, gravel, eggshells, coffee grounds), SG ~2.65. Causes abrasion and accumulation.

**48. FRONT:** Grit channel target velocity?

> **BACK:** About 0.3 m/s (1 ft/s).

**49. FRONT:** Grit is greasy and septic-smelling — why?

> **BACK:** Velocity too low (or air too low in aerated chamber) — organics settling with grit.

**50. FRONT:** Aerated grit chamber: effect of more air?

> **BACK:** More air = stronger roll = less grit captured (and fewer organics). Less air = more organics settle.

**51. FRONT:** Adding a second grit channel does what to velocity?

> **BACK:** Lowers it (more area, same flow: V = Q ÷ A).

**52. FRONT:** Screenings handling?

> **BACK:** Wash/compact, store covered, dispose (usually landfill). Watch for odours, flies, sharps.

## Primary treatment (Module 4)

**53. FRONT:** What does a primary clarifier remove?

> **BACK:** Settleable solids (sludge) and floatables (scum) by gravity.

**54. FRONT:** Typical primary removals?

> **BACK:** TSS ~50–70%, BOD ~25–40%.

**55. FRONT:** Detention time formula and typical primary DT?

> **BACK:** DT = Volume ÷ Flow. Primary ~1.5–2.5 h.

**56. FRONT:** Surface overflow rate?

> **BACK:** Flow ÷ surface area. Higher SOR (Surface Overflow Rate) = poorer settling.

**57. FRONT:** Weir overflow rate?

> **BACK:** Flow ÷ weir length (circular weir = 3.14 × D). High WOR (Weir Overflow Rate) pulls solids over weirs.

**58. FRONT:** Too little primary sludge pumping causes?

> **BACK:** Septic, gasified floating sludge; carryover; high collector torque.

**59. FRONT:** Too much primary sludge pumping causes?

> **BACK:** Thin, watery sludge; hydraulic overload of thickeners/digesters ('coning').

**60. FRONT:** Short-circuiting causes?

> **BACK:** Un-level weirs, damaged baffles, temperature/density currents, wind. Detect with a dye test.

**61. FRONT:** Typical primary sludge solids?

> **BACK:** About 2–6%.

## Biological treatment (Module 5)

**62. FRONT:** Aerobic / anoxic / anaerobic?

> **BACK:** Aerobic: DO present. Anoxic: no DO, nitrate present. Anaerobic: no DO, no nitrate.

**63. FRONT:** Facultative bacteria?

> **BACK:** Can live with or without oxygen.

**64. FRONT:** Activated sludge flow?

> **BACK:** Influent → aeration (mixed liquor) → secondary clarifier → RAS (Return Activated Sludge) back / WAS (Waste Activated Sludge) out → effluent.

**65. FRONT:** Target aeration DO?

> **BACK:** About 1–3 mg/L (2 mg/L common).

**66. FRONT:** MLSS (Mixed Liquor Suspended Solids) vs MLVSS (Mixed Liquor Volatile Suspended Solids)?

> **BACK:** MLSS = total solids in aeration. MLVSS = volatile (organic) part ≈ living biomass, ~70–80% of MLSS.

**67. FRONT:** RAS vs WAS?

> **BACK:** RAS returns sludge to aeration (controls WHERE sludge is/blanket). WAS removes sludge (controls HOW MUCH — MLSS, SRT (Solids Retention Time), F/M).

**68. FRONT:** Waste MORE sludge →?

> **BACK:** MLSS ↓, SRT ↓, F/M ↑ (younger sludge).

**69. FRONT:** Waste LESS sludge →?

> **BACK:** MLSS ↑, SRT ↑, F/M ↓ (older sludge).

**70. FRONT:** F/M ratio?

> **BACK:** kg BOD/d entering aeration ÷ kg MLVSS in aeration. High = young; low = old.

**71. FRONT:** SRT / sludge age?

> **BACK:** Average days bugs stay in the system = kg solids in system ÷ kg/d leaving (WAS + effluent).

**72. FRONT:** SVI (Sludge Volume Index)?

> **BACK:** SSV₃₀ (Settled Sludge Volume after 30 minutes) × 1,000 ÷ MLSS. ~50–150 good; >150–200 bulking.

**73. FRONT:** Extended aeration features?

> **BACK:** Low F/M (~0.05–0.15), long SRT (20–30+ d), long HRT (18–36 h), little sludge. Includes oxidation ditches/package plants.

**74. FRONT:** SBR (Sequencing Batch Reactor) steps?

> **BACK:** Fill, React, Settle, Decant, Idle ('Fat Rats Sit Down Inside').

**75. FRONT:** Causes of filamentous bulking?

> **BACK:** Low DO, low F/M, nutrient deficiency, septic/sulphides, low pH, grease.

**76. FRONT:** White billowy foam means?

> **BACK:** Young sludge / low MLSS / start-up (or detergents/toxicity) → reduce wasting.

**77. FRONT:** Thick brown greasy foam means?

> **BACK:** Old sludge / foaming organisms (Nocardia) / grease → increase wasting; remove foam from system.

**78. FRONT:** Black mixed liquor, rotten-egg odour?

> **BACK:** Septic/anaerobic — not enough DO.

**79. FRONT:** Rising sludge?

> **BACK:** Sludge settles then floats with N₂ bubbles — denitrification in clarifier. Increase RAS.

**80. FRONT:** Pin floc indicates?

> **BACK:** Old sludge (long SRT) or over-aeration.

**81. FRONT:** Straggler/dispersed floc indicates?

> **BACK:** Young sludge (high F/M), toxicity or start-up.

**82. FRONT:** Microorganism progression young → old?

> **BACK:** Amoebae/flagellates → free-swimming ciliates → stalked ciliates → rotifers → nematodes.

**83. FRONT:** Sudden DO rise with no air change + worse effluent?

> **BACK:** Possible toxic upset — bugs stopped using oxygen.

**84. FRONT:** How fast to change wasting?

> **BACK:** Gradually — about 10–15% per day; one change at a time.

**85. FRONT:** Lagoon DO/pH daily pattern?

> **BACK:** Highest late afternoon (photosynthesis); lowest just before dawn.

**86. FRONT:** Lagoon colours?

> **BACK:** Green = healthy; pink/red = purple sulphur bacteria (anaerobic/overloaded); grey/black = septic; blue-green scum = cyanobacteria.

**87. FRONT:** Trickling filter problems?

> **BACK:** Ponding, filter flies, odours, icing, sloughing. Recirculation helps many.

**88. FRONT:** RBC (Rotating Biological Contactor) normal vs white biomass?

> **BACK:** ~40% submerged. Brown/grey shaggy = normal. White = sulphur bacteria (overload, H₂S, low DO).

## Secondary clarification (Module 6)

**89. FRONT:** Secondary clarifier purpose?

> **BACK:** Settle biological floc: clear effluent out, RAS back, WAS out.

**90. FRONT:** Effect of increasing RAS?

> **BACK:** Blanket drops; RAS thinner; more hydraulic and solids load on clarifier.

**91. FRONT:** RAS %?

> **BACK:** RAS flow ÷ influent flow × 100.

**92. FRONT:** Target blanket?

> **BACK:** Low and stable — commonly ~0.3–0.9 m (1–3 ft).

**93. FRONT:** Bulking vs rising vs washout?

> **BACK:** Bulking: settles slowly (high SVI). Rising: settles then floats. Washout: only during storms; settleometer normal.

**94. FRONT:** Rising blanket, thin RAS, normal settleometer?

> **BACK:** RAS pump/line or collector failure — check equipment first.

**95. FRONT:** Secondary vs primary SOR?

> **BACK:** Secondary is lower (~16–32 m³/m²·d (cubic metres per square metre per day)) because floc settles slower.

**96. FRONT:** Algae on weirs problem?

> **BACK:** Breaks off → higher TSS; uneven weirs → short-circuiting.

## Nutrient removal (Module 7)

**97. FRONT:** Nitrogen pathway?

> **BACK:** Organic N → ammonia → nitrite → nitrate → N₂ gas.

**98. FRONT:** TKN (Total Kjeldahl Nitrogen)?

> **BACK:** Organic N + ammonia N.

**99. FRONT:** Nitrification?

> **BACK:** Ammonia → nitrite (Nitrosomonas) → nitrate (Nitrobacter). Aerobic.

**100. FRONT:** Nitrifier needs?

> **BACK:** DO ≥ ~2 mg/L, long SRT, adequate alkalinity, pH ~7–8, warm temps, no toxics.

**101. FRONT:** Nitrification costs?

> **BACK:** ≈4.6 mg O₂ and ≈7.1 mg alkalinity (as CaCO₃) per mg ammonia-N.

**102. FRONT:** Denitrification?

> **BACK:** Nitrate → N₂ gas. Needs anoxic (no DO), nitrate, carbon (BOD). Recovers some alkalinity.

**103. FRONT:** Nitrite and chlorine?

> **BACK:** Nitrite consumes chlorine (~5 mg Cl₂/mg NO₂-N (nitrite measured as nitrogen)) → residual disappears, disinfection fails.

**104. FRONT:** How is phosphorus removed from the system?

> **BACK:** Only by wasting sludge (bio-P or chemical precipitate).

**105. FRONT:** Chemical P removal chemicals and side effects?

> **BACK:** Alum, ferric chloride/sulphate. Consume alkalinity (lower pH), make more sludge.

**106. FRONT:** Bio-P zones?

> **BACK:** Anaerobic (PAOs (Phosphorus-Accumulating Organisms) release P, take up VFAs (Volatile Fatty Acids)) → aerobic (luxury P uptake) → removed in WAS. Nitrate in anaerobic zone ruins it.

**107. FRONT:** Secondary phosphorus release?

> **BACK:** P-rich sludge going anaerobic (blankets, digesters, storage) releases P back to water and recycle streams.

## Disinfection (Module 8)

**108. FRONT:** Dose, demand, residual?

> **BACK:** Dose = Demand + Residual. Residual is what's left after the water 'uses up' chlorine.

**109. FRONT:** Why dose ≠ residual?

> **BACK:** Ammonia, nitrite, organics, solids, H₂S, iron and manganese consume chlorine first.

**110. FRONT:** HOCl (hypochlorous acid) vs OCl⁻ (hypochlorite ion)?

> **BACK:** HOCl (hypochlorous acid) ~80–100× stronger. Lower pH → more HOCl → better disinfection.

**111. FRONT:** Free vs combined chlorine?

> **BACK:** Free = HOCl + OCl⁻. Combined = chloramines (weaker, longer-lasting). Total = free + combined.

**112. FRONT:** CT?

> **BACK:** Concentration (mg/L) × contact time (min).

**113. FRONT:** Typical chlorine contact time?

> **BACK:** 30 minutes is the typical industry standard (WPI (Water Professionals International)'s own sample answer); baffled tank prevents short-circuiting.

**114. FRONT:** Chlorine product strengths?

> **BACK:** Gas 100%; calcium hypochlorite ~65–70%; sodium hypochlorite ~10–15% (commonly 12%).

**115. FRONT:** Sodium hypochlorite storage?

> **BACK:** Loses strength with heat, sunlight, time; off-gasses (pump airlock).

**116. FRONT:** Chlorine withdrawal limits?

> **BACK:** ~18 kg/d from 68 kg cylinder; ~180 kg/d from ton. Frost = too fast. Never heat with flame.

**117. FRONT:** Ton container valves?

> **BACK:** Top valve = gas; bottom valve = liquid.

**118. FRONT:** Why dechlorinate and with what?

> **BACK:** Residual chlorine is toxic to fish. Use sulphur dioxide or sodium bisulphite. Federal TRC (Total Residual Chlorine) limit 0.02 mg/L.

**119. FRONT:** UV disinfection basics?

> **BACK:** ~254 nm damages DNA; no residual; needs clear water; clean sleeves, replace lamps; low UVT/TSS reduce effect.

**120. FRONT:** Chlorine residual test?

> **BACK:** DPD (N,N-diethyl-p-phenylenediamine, chlorine test reagent) — turns pink. Test immediately.

## Sludge & biosolids (Module 9)

**121. FRONT:** Solids handling goals?

> **BACK:** Reduce volume (remove water), stabilize (odour, pathogens, vectors), dispose/beneficially use.

**122. FRONT:** 1% solids = ? mg/L

> **BACK:** 10,000 mg/L.

**123. FRONT:** Typical solids: primary, WAS, cake?

> **BACK:** Primary ~2–6%; WAS ~0.5–1.5%; dewatered cake ~15–30%.

**124. FRONT:** Gravity thickener vs DAF (Dissolved Air Flotation)?

> **BACK:** Gravity thickener → primary sludge. DAF (tiny bubbles float solids) → WAS.

**125. FRONT:** Anaerobic digestion two stages?

> **BACK:** Acid formers (fast, tough) → methane formers (slow, sensitive).

**126. FRONT:** Mesophilic digester temperature?

> **BACK:** ~35 °C (95 °F); change ≤ ~1 °C/day.

**127. FRONT:** Healthy digester indicators?

> **BACK:** pH ~6.8–7.2; VA/ALK (Volatile Acids to Alkalinity ratio) < ~0.1–0.2; gas ~60–70% methane, ~30–35% CO₂ (carbon dioxide).

**128. FRONT:** Sour digester sequence?

> **BACK:** VA/ALK rises → alkalinity drops → pH drops (last) → gas drops, CO₂ rises. Cut feed; add alkalinity.

**129. FRONT:** Digester gas safety devices?

> **BACK:** Flame arrestors, pressure/vacuum relief valves, drip traps, waste gas burner.

**130. FRONT:** Aerobic digester pH drop?

> **BACK:** Nitrification consumes alkalinity. Keep DO ≥ ~1 mg/L.

**131. FRONT:** Belt press zones?

> **BACK:** Gravity drainage → wedge → pressure/shear.

**132. FRONT:** Polymer dose too low / too high?

> **BACK:** Too low: dirty filtrate, wet cake. Too high: slimy sludge, blinded belts, wasted money.

**133. FRONT:** Return streams?

> **BACK:** Supernatant, filtrate, centrate — strong in BOD/TSS/NH₃/P. Return slowly.

**134. FRONT:** BC biosolids regulation?

> **BACK:** Organic Matter Recycling Regulation (OMRR).

## Pumps (Module 10)

**135. FRONT:** Centrifugal vs PD (Positive Displacement) pumps?

> **BACK:** Centrifugal: spinning impeller; flow drops as head rises. PD: fixed volume per stroke/turn; nearly constant flow.

**136. FRONT:** PD pump with closed discharge?

> **BACK:** Pressure builds until something breaks — never do it; relief valve required.

**137. FRONT:** Impeller?

> **BACK:** Spinning part that throws water outward; enters at the eye. Clogging/wear reduce flow.

**138. FRONT:** Wear rings?

> **BACK:** Keep tight clearance between impeller and casing; worn → internal recirculation → less flow/pressure.

**139. FRONT:** Packing leakage?

> **BACK:** Slow steady drip (~20–60 drops/min). Too tight = burned packing, scored sleeve.

**140. FRONT:** Mechanical seal leakage?

> **BACK:** None. A leaking seal has failed. Never run dry.

**141. FRONT:** Lantern ring and seal water?

> **BACK:** Lantern ring distributes seal water; seal water slightly above stuffing box pressure.

**142. FRONT:** Cavitation (simple)?

> **BACK:** Low suction pressure → water boils into vapour bubbles → bubbles collapse violently → gravel sound, pitting.

**143. FRONT:** Fix cavitation?

> **BACK:** Fully open/clear suction, raise wet-well level, reduce suction lift, reduce speed. Never throttle suction.

**144. FRONT:** Priming?

> **BACK:** Filling pump/suction with liquid; centrifugal pumps can't pump air. Foot valve holds prime.

**145. FRONT:** Air binding fix?

> **BACK:** Vent air; fix suction leaks; raise level (prevent vortexing).

**146. FRONT:** Suction lift vs suction head?

> **BACK:** Lift: source below pump. Head (flooded): source above pump.

**147. FRONT:** TDH (Total Dynamic Head)?

> **BACK:** Total dynamic head = static head + friction losses (+ velocity head).

**148. FRONT:** Pump curve basics?

> **BACK:** Head ↑ → flow ↓. Shutoff head at zero flow. Run near BEP (Best Efficiency Point).

**149. FRONT:** Parallel vs series pumps?

> **BACK:** Parallel adds flow; series adds head.

**150. FRONT:** Wrong rotation (3-phase)?

> **BACK:** Pump gives low flow; swap any two leads.

**151. FRONT:** Pump spins backward after stopping?

> **BACK:** Leaking/failed check valve.

**152. FRONT:** Short cycling causes?

> **BACK:** Float settings too close, leaking check valve, oversized pump. Overheats motor.

**153. FRONT:** Misalignment results?

> **BACK:** Vibration, bearing and seal failure.

**154. FRONT:** Over-greasing?

> **BACK:** Overheats bearings and can damage seals.

## Valves & piping (Module 11)

**155. FRONT:** Gate valve use?

> **BACK:** Isolation — fully open or fully closed. Don't throttle.

**156. FRONT:** Globe valve use?

> **BACK:** Throttling; high headloss; flow arrow.

**157. FRONT:** Quarter-turn valves?

> **BACK:** Ball, butterfly, plug. Butterfly common on air headers; eccentric plug common for sludge.

**158. FRONT:** Check valve?

> **BACK:** One-way flow; prevents backflow; watch flow arrow; slam → water hammer. Ball checks common in lift stations.

**159. FRONT:** Knife gate / pinch / diaphragm valves?

> **BACK:** Knife gate & pinch → sludge/slurries. Diaphragm → chemicals.

**160. FRONT:** Telescoping valve?

> **BACK:** Adjustable-level gravity draw-off of sludge or supernatant.

**161. FRONT:** Air release valve location?

> **BACK:** High points of force mains (air rises).

**162. FRONT:** Pressure relief valve required on?

> **BACK:** Positive displacement pump discharges.

**163. FRONT:** Fail-open / fail-closed?

> **BACK:** Position a control valve goes to on loss of power/air.

**164. FRONT:** Best backflow protection?

> **BACK:** Air gap. RP assembly for high hazard where no air gap.

**165. FRONT:** Back-pressure vs back-siphonage?

> **BACK:** Back-pressure: downstream pressure higher than supply. Back-siphonage: supply pressure drops.

**166. FRONT:** Why exercise valves?

> **BACK:** So they don't seize and will work in an emergency; count turns.

## Hydraulics (Module 12)

**167. FRONT:** Q = A × V?

> **BACK:** Flow = area × velocity. V = Q ÷ A; A = Q ÷ V.

**168. FRONT:** Continuity?

> **BACK:** A₁V₁ = A₂V₂ — smaller area, faster water. Half diameter → ¼ area → 4× velocity.

**169. FRONT:** Pressure–head conversions?

> **BACK:** 1 m ≈ 9.81 kPa (kilopascal); 1 psi (pounds per square inch) = 2.31 ft; 1 ft = 0.433 psi.

**170. FRONT:** What sets pressure at a tank bottom?

> **BACK:** Depth only — not width or shape.

**171. FRONT:** What increases friction headloss?

> **BACK:** Longer pipe, smaller diameter, higher velocity, rougher pipe, more fittings, partly closed valves.

**172. FRONT:** Self-cleansing sewer velocity?

> **BACK:** About 0.6 m/s (2 ft/s).

**173. FRONT:** Pressure up, flow down at a pump?

> **BACK:** Restriction downstream (closed valve, plugged/scaled force main).

**174. FRONT:** Water hammer?

> **BACK:** Pressure spike from suddenly stopping moving water. Close valves slowly.

## Instrumentation & SCADA (Module 13)

**175. FRONT:** 4–20 mA (milliamps) meaning?

> **BACK:** 4 mA = 0%, 20 mA = 100%, 12 mA = 50%. 0 mA = fault (broken wire/power).

**176. FRONT:** Mag meter needs?

> **BACK:** Full pipe, conductive liquid. No moving parts, no headloss.

**177. FRONT:** Doppler vs transit-time ultrasonic?

> **BACK:** Doppler needs particles/bubbles (sludge). Transit-time for clean liquids.

**178. FRONT:** Parshall flume?

> **BACK:** Open-channel primary device; upstream level gives flow; self-cleaning.

**179. FRONT:** Ultrasonic level errors?

> **BACK:** Foam, condensation, obstructions, turbulence.

**180. FRONT:** Bubbler errors?

> **BACK:** Plugged tube reads HIGH; lost air reads low/zero.

**181. FRONT:** pH calibration and storage?

> **BACK:** Buffers 7 then 4/10 bracketing sample; store wet in storage solution.

**182. FRONT:** Fouled DO probe reads?

> **BACK:** Low → controller over-aerates (wastes energy). Verify with portable meter.

**183. FRONT:** ORP (Oxidation-Reduction Potential)?

> **BACK:** mV (millivolts); + oxidizing (O₂, chlorine); − reducing (anoxic/anaerobic).

**184. FRONT:** HOA switch?

> **BACK:** Hand = manual (may bypass controls); Off = stopped but NOT lockout; Auto = automatic control.

**185. FRONT:** SCADA (Supervisory Control And Data Acquisition), PLC (Programmable Logic Controller), HMI (Human-Machine Interface), telemetry?

> **BACK:** SCADA supervises; PLC runs local logic; HMI = screens; telemetry = remote communication.

**186. FRONT:** Alarm response?

> **BACK:** Acknowledge → verify → respond per SOP (Standard Operating Procedure) → record. Never just silence.

**187. FRONT:** Calibration zero and span?

> **BACK:** Zero = bottom of range; span = top of range. Record as-found/as-left.

**188. FRONT:** Suspect instrument reading?

> **BACK:** Verify independently before changing the process.

**189. FRONT:** SCADA security?

> **BACK:** Own login, never share passwords, lock screens, no personal USBs, report odd behaviour.

## Electrical & mechanical (Module 14)

**190. FRONT:** Voltage / current / resistance / power?

> **BACK:** Voltage = push (V); current = flow (A); resistance = opposition (Ω); power = rate of work (W).

**191. FRONT:** Ohm's law and power?

> **BACK:** V = I × R; P = V × I.

**192. FRONT:** hp ↔ kW (kilowatt)?

> **BACK:** 1 hp = 0.746 kW; 1 kW = 1.34 hp.

**193. FRONT:** Fuse/breaker vs overload vs GFCI (Ground-Fault Circuit Interrupter)?

> **BACK:** Fuse/breaker protect wiring (short circuits). Overload protects motor (sustained over-current). GFCI protects people.

**194. FRONT:** Replacing a blown fuse?

> **BACK:** Same type and rating, after finding the cause. Never oversize or bypass.

**195. FRONT:** Single-phasing?

> **BACK:** Loss of one of three phases → motor overheats/burns out.

**196. FRONT:** VFD (Variable Frequency Drive)?

> **BACK:** Variable frequency drive — changes motor speed.

**197. FRONT:** Grounding purpose?

> **BACK:** Safe path for fault current so protection trips.

**198. FRONT:** Test for absence of voltage?

> **BACK:** Live–dead–live (test tester, test circuit, test tester).

**199. FRONT:** Standby generator care?

> **BACK:** Exercise regularly under load; check fuel, oil, coolant, battery, block heater; ATS (Automatic Transfer Switch) transfers load; UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) keeps PLC/SCADA alive.

**200. FRONT:** 5 rights of lubrication?

> **BACK:** Right lubricant, amount, place, time, method.

**201. FRONT:** Shear pin?

> **BACK:** Breaks to protect a drive when jammed.

**202. FRONT:** Mixers — where and why?

> **BACK:** Anoxic zones, EQ basins, digesters, chemical flash/flocculation mixing. Keep solids suspended without adding air; watch for ragging.

**203. FRONT:** What is an MCC (Motor Control Centre)?

> **BACK:** Motor Control Center — cabinet of 'buckets', each with a motor's disconnect, starter and overload. Common lockout point.

**204. FRONT:** Conveyor and hoist safety?

> **BACK:** Lock out conveyors before clearing jams (pull-cords are e-stops, not lockout). Hoists: rated capacity, inspect rigging, never stand under a load.

**205. FRONT:** Maintenance types?

> **BACK:** Preventive (scheduled), predictive (condition-based), corrective (after failure), emergency.

**206. FRONT:** Rising blower pressure + falling airflow?

> **BACK:** Fouled/clogged diffusers.

## Laboratory & sampling (Module 15)

**207. FRONT:** Representative sample?

> **BACK:** Well-mixed location, consistent time/place, avoid scum and deposits, clean equipment.

**208. FRONT:** Grab-only tests?

> **BACK:** pH, temperature, DO, chlorine residual, bacteria, oil & grease, sulphide.

**209. FRONT:** Best composite for loads?

> **BACK:** Flow-proportional, kept ≤ 6 °C.

**210. FRONT:** Preservation: cool vs acid?

> **BACK:** Cool most samples. H₂SO₄ (sulphuric acid) to pH < 2 for COD, ammonia, TKN, total P.

**211. FRONT:** Holding times to remember?

> **BACK:** pH, temp, DO, Cl₂ (chlorine gas) residual: immediately. BOD: 48 h. Bacteria: ~8 h.

**212. FRONT:** Bacteria bottle contents?

> **BACK:** Sterile with sodium thiosulphate (neutralizes chlorine). Don't rinse.

**213. FRONT:** Chain of custody?

> **BACK:** Signed record of everyone who handled a sample from collection to analysis.

**214. FRONT:** QC (Quality Control) checks?

> **BACK:** Calibration, blanks (contamination), duplicates (precision), standards (accuracy), control charts, records.

**215. FRONT:** BOD validity rules?

> **BACK:** Depletion ≥ 2.0 mg/L; residual ≥ 1.0 mg/L; blank ≤ 0.2 mg/L; GGA (Glucose–Glutamic Acid, BOD check standard) check standard.

**216. FRONT:** BOD formula?

> **BACK:** (DOi − DOf) × 300 ÷ mL sample.

**217. FRONT:** TSS formula?

> **BACK:** (dry wt − filter wt, g) × 1,000,000 ÷ mL sample.

**218. FRONT:** TSS vs VSS (Volatile Suspended Solids) temperatures?

> **BACK:** TSS dried at 103–105 °C; VSS burned at 550 °C.

**219. FRONT:** Settleable solids test?

> **BACK:** Imhoff cone, 60 minutes, mL/L.

**220. FRONT:** Alkalinity test?

> **BACK:** Acid titration to ~pH 4.5; as CaCO₃.

**221. FRONT:** Phosphate colour test?

> **BACK:** Ascorbic acid method — blue.

**222. FRONT:** WET test?

> **BACK:** Whole Effluent Toxicity — test organisms exposed to effluent; acute = survival over a short time. Failures often from chlorine residual or ammonia.

**223. FRONT:** Metals sample preservation?

> **BACK:** Nitric acid to pH < 2 in clean plastic; metals inhibit biology and accumulate in biosolids.

**224. FRONT:** Lab safety basics?

> **BACK:** PPE, acid to water, fume hood, never mouth-pipette, no food, COD waste is hazardous (mercury/chromium).

## Troubleshooting (Module 16)

**225. FRONT:** Troubleshooting order?

> **BACK:** Safety → verify → gather info → list causes → check equipment first → small change → notify → monitor & record.

**226. FRONT:** High effluent TSS first checks?

> **BACK:** Blanket, settleometer pattern, flow, RAS pump, SVI, weirs.

**227. FRONT:** High effluent ammonia in winter?

> **BACK:** Reduce wasting (↑ SRT); keep DO ≥ 2; check alkalinity/pH; toxics.

**228. FRONT:** Low DO even with all blowers on?

> **BACK:** Verify probe; check diffusers (fouling), overload, blower/valve problems.

**229. FRONT:** Chlorine residual suddenly zero?

> **BACK:** Feed failure (empty cylinder, airlock, ejector) or demand spike (nitrite, solids).

**230. FRONT:** Lift station pump tripped at night?

> **BACK:** Run standby pump to control level, then investigate safely; reset once only; report overflows.

## Administration & BC regulations (Module 18)

**231. FRONT:** Exam breakdown (2025 NTK (Need-to-Know Criteria))?

> **BACK:** 100 Qs: Equipment 39, Process 38, Safety/Admin 13, Lab 10. ~40% recall, ~60% application, 10 calculation Qs (9 process, 1 lab). US units first, metric in brackets.

**232. FRONT:** Vulnerability assessment?

> **BACK:** Identify threats (natural, accidental, deliberate/cyber) to critical assets and plan protections; feeds the ERP (Emergency Response Plan).

**233. FRONT:** Correcting a logbook error?

> **BACK:** Single line through it, write correction, initial and date. Never erase or falsify.

**234. FRONT:** What must be reported?

> **BACK:** Spills, overflows, bypasses, permit exceedances, injuries and near misses — per procedure/regulation.

**235. FRONT:** SOP vs O&M (Operations and Maintenance) manual vs ERP?

> **BACK:** SOP = step-by-step task. O&M = how the plant works/maintained. ERP = emergency procedures and contacts.

**236. FRONT:** BC law requiring EOCP (Environmental Operators Certification Program)-certified operators?

> **BACK:** Municipal Wastewater Regulation (B.C. Reg. 87/2012), s.47, under the Environmental Management Act.

**237. FRONT:** Federal effluent regulation?

> **BACK:** Wastewater Systems Effluent Regulations (Fisheries Act): e.g., CBOD 25, SS 25, TRC 0.02 mg/L, un-ionized ammonia 1.25 mg/L.

**238. FRONT:** Who regulates worker safety in BC?

> **BACK:** WorkSafeBC (Workers Compensation Act + OHS (Occupational Health and Safety) Regulation).

**239. FRONT:** What is EOCP?

> **BACK:** Environmental Operators Certification Program — certifies operators and classifies facilities in BC and Yukon.

**240. FRONT:** Level I requirements?

> **BACK:** ~12 months (1,800 h) experience, high school or equivalent, pass the WPI exam (70%), live/work in BC or Yukon.

**241. FRONT:** Sewer-use bylaw?

> **BACK:** Local rule controlling what businesses/industries can discharge to sewers (source control).

**242. FRONT:** Media asks about an incident?

> **BACK:** Refer to the designated spokesperson.

## Tertiary, effluent & odour (Module 19)

**243. FRONT:** Coagulation vs flocculation?

> **BACK:** Coagulation: coagulant (alum/ferric) neutralizes particle charges — rapid mix. Flocculation: polymer bridges particles into flocs — slow mix. Dose by jar test.

**244. FRONT:** Nutrient enhancement?

> **BACK:** Adding N or P when wastewater is short of nutrients (BOD:N:P ≈ 100:5:1), e.g., food-processing waste.

**245. FRONT:** When is a tertiary filter backwashed?

> **BACK:** When headloss reaches the set point, turbidity breaks through, or the run time ends. Backwash water returns to the plant.

**246. FRONT:** Filters backwashing far too often?

> **BACK:** Too many solids coming from upstream (bulking/carryover) — fix the clarifier.

**247. FRONT:** Purpose of a flow equalization basin?

> **BACK:** Store peak flows and release an even flow/load; keep it mixed/aerated so it doesn't go septic.

**248. FRONT:** Main cause of odours at plants?

> **BACK:** Hydrogen sulphide (H₂S) from septic wastewater — toxic and corrosive.

**249. FRONT:** Odour control methods?

> **BACK:** Covers + biofilters (keep moist), chemical scrubbers (caustic/hypochlorite), activated carbon, chemical addition (iron salts, nitrate, peroxide), pre-aeration.

**250. FRONT:** Rapid infiltration basin care?

> **BACK:** Alternate loading and resting; let dry and scarify the surface to restore infiltration.

**251. FRONT:** Reclaimed water rules of thumb?

> **BACK:** Higher treatment/disinfection; never cross-connect to drinking water; commonly purple pipe.

**252. FRONT:** Where do screenings and grit go?

> **BACK:** Landfill (per local rules). Sludge → biosolids (OMRR) or landfill. Side-streams → back to the plant.

## Math formulas (Module 17)

**253. FRONT:** Area of a circle?

> **BACK:** 0.785 × D² (= 3.14 × r²). π = 3.14 on EOCP exams.

**254. FRONT:** Circumference?

> **BACK:** 3.14 × D (use for circular weir length).

**255. FRONT:** Volume of a cylinder?

> **BACK:** 0.785 × D² × depth.

**256. FRONT:** Detention time?

> **BACK:** Volume ÷ Flow (× 24 if flow is per day and you want hours).

**257. FRONT:** SOR / WOR?

> **BACK:** SOR = Flow ÷ surface area. WOR = Flow ÷ weir length.

**258. FRONT:** kg/day formula?

> **BACK:** mg/L × m³/d (cubic metres per day) ÷ 1,000 (= mg/L × ML/d).

**259. FRONT:** lb/day formula?

> **BACK:** mg/L × MGD (Million Gallons per Day) × 8.34.

**260. FRONT:** Chemical with purity?

> **BACK:** kg/d of product = kg/d active ÷ purity (decimal) — answer gets bigger.

**261. FRONT:** % removal?

> **BACK:** (In − Out) ÷ In × 100.

**262. FRONT:** Dilution?

> **BACK:** C₁V₁ = C₂V₂.

**263. FRONT:** Thickening volume?

> **BACK:** V₂ = V₁ × %₁ ÷ %₂.

**264. FRONT:** Dry solids in sludge (metric)?

> **BACK:** kg/d = m³/d × % × 10.

**265. FRONT:** Pump water power?

> **BACK:** Metric: 9.81 × Q(m³/s) × H(m) kW. US: gpm (gallons per minute) × ft ÷ 3,960 whp (water horsepower). Divide by efficiencies for brake/motor.

**266. FRONT:** Key conversions?

> **BACK:** 1 m³ = 1,000 L; 1 L/s (litres per second) = 86.4 m³/d; 1 ft³ = 7.48 gal; 1 gal = 8.34 lb; 1 MGD = 694.4 gpm; 1 day = 1,440 min.

**267. FRONT:** Temperature conversion?

> **BACK:** °F = °C × 1.8 + 32; °C = (°F − 32) ÷ 1.8.
