# Phase 4 — Mixed Practice (60 questions)

← [Course index](../README.md) · [Exam Prep System](exam-prep-system.md)

Questions from **every** module, shuffled so you can't predict the topic. Weighted roughly like the real exam (23 Equipment · 23 Process · 8 Safety/Admin · 6 Lab). Includes BC-specific items from Module 18. Original practice items — **not** actual exam questions.

**Exam conditions:**
- **Time:** about 1 hour 45 minutes (practise under a timer).
- **Allowed:** calculator and the [formula/conversion sheet](../math/formula-and-conversion-sheet.md) (or better, the official WPI sheet). **Nothing else** — no notes.
- **One best answer** per question. Answer every question (no penalty for guessing — confirm with EOCP).
- Write your answers on paper (1–60), then check them against the key at the bottom.
- **π = 3.14.**

---

## Questions

**1.** A chlorine contact tank holds 180 m³. Flow is 0.1 m³/s. What is the contact time?

   A. 18 minutes  
   B. 0.5 minutes  
   C. 30 minutes  
   D. 1,800 minutes  

**2.** Which maintenance approach uses vibration readings and oil analysis to decide when to repair equipment?

   A. Predictive (condition-based) maintenance  
   B. No maintenance  
   C. Corrective maintenance only  
   D. Emergency maintenance  

**3.** A chlorine feed rate that automatically increases when plant flow increases is an example of:

   A. Flow-paced (proportional) control  
   B. Time-of-day control  
   C. Manual control  
   D. On/off float control  

**4.** Olfactory fatigue means that:

   A. Exposure to H₂S can deaden the sense of smell, hiding the danger  
   B. Chlorine becomes odourless  
   C. Methane smells stronger over time  
   D. Gas detectors stop working  

**5.** A pipe fitting that allows two pipes to be easily disconnected later is a:

   A. Reducer  
   B. Union  
   C. Tee  
   D. Cap  

**6.** When repacking a pump, the joints (cut ends) of the packing rings should be:

   A. All lined up at the bottom  
   B. All lined up at the top  
   C. Glued together  
   D. Staggered around the shaft (e.g., 90–120° apart)  

**7.** The discs of a rotating biological contactor are normally submerged about:

   A. 90%  
   B. 100%  
   C. 5%  
   D. 40%  

**8.** A 150 mm pipe flows full at 0.75 m/s. What is the flow?

   A. 13.2 L/s  
   B. 132 L/s  
   C. 1.3 L/s  
   D. 0.013 L/s  

**9.** A lab result was lost because the sample bottle broke. The correct action is to:

   A. Use last week's result  
   B. Estimate a likely value  
   C. Leave it blank with no explanation  
   D. Record the result as missing with the reason and notify the supervisor  

**10.** Which of the following must be collected as a grab sample?

   A. Dissolved oxygen  
   B. 24-hour total phosphorus  
   C. 24-hour BOD  
   D. 24-hour TSS  

**11.** When adjusting the WAS rate, a good practice is to change it by no more than about:

   A. 50% per day  
   B. It doesn't matter  
   C. 10–15% per day  
   D. 100% per day  

**12.** An arc flash is best described as:

   A. A pump cavitation event  
   B. A type of fuse  
   C. An explosive release of electrical energy causing intense heat, light and pressure  
   D. A slow electrical leak to ground  

**13.** During spring snowmelt, heavy infiltration and inflow will usually cause:

   A. No change  
   B. Higher flows and more dilute (lower mg/L) wastewater  
   C. Lower flows and stronger wastewater  
   D. Higher temperature  

**14.** A pressure gauge on a raw sludge line keeps plugging. The best fix is to install a:

   A. Larger gauge  
   B. Rotameter  
   C. Globe valve  
   D. Diaphragm seal (gauge isolator)  

**15.** A pressure gauge on a positive displacement pump discharge bounces wildly. A device to dampen this is a:

   A. Foot valve  
   B. Lantern ring  
   C. Snubber (or liquid-filled gauge)  
   D. Check valve  

**16.** Pre-aeration at the headworks is used mainly to:

   A. Freshen septic wastewater and reduce odours  
   B. Thicken sludge  
   C. Remove phosphorus  
   D. Disinfect  

**17.** For a V-notch weir to measure flow accurately, the operator should:

   A. Paint over the notch  
   B. Submerge the weir  
   C. Keep the weir crest clean and sharp and keep solids from building up behind it  
   D. Let algae grow on the crest  

**18.** Odours from a lagoon are most common:

   A. Never  
   B. Right after mowing the dikes  
   C. In mid-summer afternoons  
   D. In spring, around ice-off and turnover  

**19.** Methane tends to collect:

   A. Only outdoors  
   B. At the floor because it is heavier than air  
   C. Near the top of enclosed spaces because it is lighter than air  
   D. Only in chlorine rooms  

**20.** Black influent with a strong rotten-egg odour indicates:

   A. Normal industrial dye  
   B. Excess chlorine  
   C. Fresh wastewater  
   D. Septic wastewater containing hydrogen sulphide  

**21.** Compared with a primary clarifier, a secondary clarifier is normally designed for a:

   A. Higher weir rate only  
   B. Shorter time only  
   C. Higher surface overflow rate  
   D. Lower surface overflow rate  

**22.** Ammonia in treated effluent is a concern mainly because it:

   A. Causes turbidity  
   B. Is toxic to fish and exerts an oxygen demand  
   C. Is a disinfectant  
   D. Increases hardness  

**23.** A 907 kg chlorine ton container is used at 55 kg/d. About how many days will it last?

   A. 1.6 days  
   B. 16.5 days  
   C. 165 days  
   D. 49,885 days  

**24.** When two operators work on the same pump, lockout requires:

   A. Each operator to apply their own personal lock  
   B. Pressing the stop button  
   C. Only a tag  
   D. One lock for both operators  

**25.** A pump motor nameplate shows full-load amps of 40 A, but the motor is drawing 48 A. This indicates:

   A. Normal operation  
   B. The pump is air-bound  
   C. The motor is lightly loaded  
   D. The motor is overloaded  

**26.** Gearbox oil looks milky. This usually means:

   A. The oil is new  
   B. The oil is too hot  
   C. Water has contaminated the oil  
   D. The gearbox is over-greased  

**27.** A clarifier drive trips repeatedly on high torque. The operator should:

   A. Increase the drive speed  
   B. Bypass the torque switch  
   C. Lock out the drive and check for heavy sludge or an obstruction before restarting  
   D. Keep resetting it  

**28.** An operator smells a solvent odour at the headworks and influent pH is 3.5. The plant's biology may be at risk. The FIRST priority is to:

   A. Protect personnel from possible hazardous vapours, then sample and notify  
   B. Shut down the blowers  
   C. Increase chlorine  
   D. Ignore it  

**29.** A reduced pressure principle (RP) backflow assembly is used for:

   A. Releasing air at high points  
   B. Measuring flow  
   C. High-hazard cross-connections where an air gap isn't practical  
   D. Throttling flow  

**30.** Effluent nitrite is high and nitrate is low in a plant that normally nitrifies fully. This suggests:

   A. Chlorine dose is too high  
   B. Nitrification is incomplete or stressed  
   C. Phosphorus removal is failing  
   D. Denitrification is excellent  

**31.** Canada's Wastewater Systems Effluent Regulations limit total residual chlorine in effluent (for systems that chlorinate) to:

   A. 2 mg/L  
   B. 0.02 mg/L  
   C. 20 mg/L  
   D. 0.5 mg/L  

**32.** Facultative bacteria are bacteria that can:

   A. Live only in chlorine  
   B. Live only without oxygen  
   C. Live only with oxygen  
   D. Live with or without dissolved oxygen  

**33.** E. coli and fecal coliforms are measured in effluent mainly because they:

   A. Indicate fecal contamination and possible pathogens  
   B. Consume chlorine  
   C. Cause bulking  
   D. Are the main cause of all waterborne disease  

**34.** The glucose–glutamic acid (GGA) standard is used in the lab to:

   A. Check the accuracy of the BOD test  
   B. Calibrate the pH meter  
   C. Measure chlorine  
   D. Preserve ammonia samples  

**35.** The BOD dilution water blank should deplete no more than about:

   A. 8.0 mg/L  
   B. 5.0 mg/L  
   C. 0.2 mg/L of DO in 5 days  
   D. 2.0 mg/L  

**36.** A pump bearing makes a grinding noise and runs hot. The most likely problem is:

   A. High wet-well level  
   B. Clean seal water  
   C. Correct operation

**37.** Influent TSS is 260 mg/L, effluent TSS is 18 mg/L, and flow is 5.5 ML/d. How many kg/d of TSS are removed?

   A. 1,430 kg/d

**38.** Water that collects in an air compressor receiver should be:

   A. Ignored

   C. Drained regularly

**39.** A pump is programmed so it cannot start when the wet-well level is below the low-level setpoint. This is an example of a(n):

   A. Snubber  
   B. Setpoint  
   C. Interlock  
   D. Totalizer  

**40.** A magnetic flow meter on a sludge line reads erratically. A common cause is:

   A. Coating of the electrodes by grease or poor grounding  
   B. There are no moving parts  
   C. The pipe is completely full

**41.** As wastewater temperature rises in summer, the maximum amount of oxygen water can hold:

   A. Doubles  
   B. Decreases  
   C. Stays the same

**42.** Trees and shrubs should be kept off lagoon dikes because:

   B. They lower the BOD

**43.** Influent flow at a municipal plant is usually lowest:
