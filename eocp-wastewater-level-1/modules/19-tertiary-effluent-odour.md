## 2. Plain-English teaching

### 2.1 Tertiary filtration

**Enters:** secondary effluent (already clear-ish, maybe 10–30 mg/L (milligrams per litre) TSS (Total Suspended Solids))
**What happens:** water passes through a **filter** (sand/anthracite media, or cloth/disc filters) that traps the fine particles the clarifier missed
**Leaves:** polished effluent (lower TSS, turbidity, phosphorus and BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) — remember solids carry BOD and P) + **backwash water** (returned to the head of the plant)
**Why it matters:** meets tighter limits, improves **UV disinfection** (clearer water = better UV), needed for **reclaimed water**

| Filter type | How it works | Cleaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Granular media filter** (sand, or dual media: anthracite over sand) | Water flows down through a bed of grains; particles get stuck between grains | **Backwash**: water (and often air scour) is pushed **upward** through the bed to lift and clean it; dirty backwash water goes back to the plant |
| **Cloth / disc filter** | Water passes through cloth on rotating discs or drums | Automatic vacuum/backwash of the cloth when headloss rises |
| **Continuous backwash (upflow) sand filter** | Sand moves slowly downward and is washed continuously | Recognize |

**Headloss** — as a filter catches solids, it becomes harder for water to pass, so the **water level above the filter rises** (or pressure difference increases). When headloss reaches a set point (or turbidity breaks through, or a timer runs out), the filter is **backwashed**.

**Common filter problems [SUPPLEMENTAL]:**
| Problem | Cause | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Short filter runs** (backwashing too often) | Too many solids from the clarifier (bulking, carryover), algae | Fix upstream solids problem |
| **Turbidity breakthrough** | Filter overloaded, end of run, media problem | Backwash; check upstream |
| **Mudballs / cracking / channelling** (granular) | Poor backwashing, grease, biological growth | Improve backwash (air scour), chlorinate/clean media |
| **Media loss** | Backwash rate too high | Reduce backwash rate; check troughs |
| **Torn cloth** (disc filters) | Wear, debris | Replace cloth panel |

### 2.2 Flow equalization (EQ) basins

**Enters:** wastewater during **peak flows** (morning peaks, storms, I/I (Infiltration and Inflow))
**What happens:** a basin **stores** the excess flow and **releases** it at a steadier rate later
**Leaves:** a more **even flow and load** to the treatment processes
**Why it matters:** prevents **hydraulic overload** and **washout** of clarifiers, keeps biology and chemical dosing stable

**Operator points:**
- Usually located **after screening and grit removal** (so rags and grit don't settle in the basin).
- **Mix and/or aerate** the basin so solids stay suspended and the contents don't go **septic** (odours, H₂S (hydrogen sulphide)).
- **Drain** the basin back into the process gradually after the storm, at low-flow times.
- Clean accumulated solids; watch level controls and pumps.

### 2.3 Odour control

**Main cause of odours:** **hydrogen sulphide (H₂S)** from **septic** (oxygen-free) wastewater — long sewer travel times, force mains, warm weather, septage, sludge handling. Other odours: ammonia, mercaptans, volatile organics. (H₂S also **corrodes** concrete and metal — it turns into sulphuric acid on damp surfaces.)

| Method | How it works | Operator checks |
|---|---|---|
| **Covers + ventilation** | Trap odorous air at headworks/sludge areas and send it to treatment | Fans running, ducts not blocked; covered areas are often confined spaces/hazardous atmospheres |
| **Biofilter** | Odorous air is blown through a moist bed of organic media (wood chips, compost); bacteria eat the H₂S | Keep media **moist** (not soaked); check pressure drop and media **pH** (H₂S makes acid); replace media when spent |
| **Chemical (wet) scrubber** | Air is washed with a chemical solution (e.g., **caustic** and/or **hypochlorite**) that absorbs/oxidizes odours | Chemical levels, pH, ORP (Oxidation-Reduction Potential), pumps, nozzles; **chemical safety** (corrosives; never mix hypochlorite with acid) |
| **Activated carbon** | Odorous compounds stick to carbon | Replace carbon when exhausted (odour breakthrough) |
| **Chemical addition in sewers/headworks** | Iron salts (bind sulphide), nitrate (prevents sulphide forming), hydrogen peroxide or hypochlorite (oxidize sulphide), magnesium hydroxide (raises pH) | Feed rates, chemical inventory |
| **Pre-aeration** | Adds oxygen to freshen septic influent | Air supply |
| **Good housekeeping** | Clean channels, remove grit/screenings and scum promptly, keep sludge from going septic | Daily |

**Odour complaints** from neighbours should be **recorded** (time, wind direction, what was happening at the plant) and reported per procedure (Module 18).

### 2.4 Effluent disposal — where the treated water goes

| Option | Description | Operator considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface water discharge (outfall)** | Pipe to a river, lake or ocean; often ends in a **diffuser** (many small ports) that mixes effluent quickly into the receiving water | Meet permit limits (BOD/CBOD, TSS, ammonia, chlorine residual, bacteria); inspect outfall; receiving-water monitoring if required |
| **Ground disposal — rapid infiltration basins (RIBs)** | Effluent is spread into shallow sand/gravel basins and **soaks into the ground**; soil provides extra treatment | **Alternate** basins (**load, then rest/dry**) so the surface can re-aerate; **scarify/rake** the surface to break up clogging layers; remove weeds; monitor groundwater wells if required |
| **Ground disposal — exfiltration/infiltration fields** | Effluent distributed through pipes buried in gravel trenches | Even distribution; watch for surfacing effluent or ponding |
| **Irrigation / land application of effluent** | Effluent used to water fields, golf courses (seasonal) | Usually needs higher treatment and disinfection; storage in non-irrigation season; buffer zones; follow permit |
| **Reclaimed water (reuse)** | Highly treated effluent reused (irrigation, toilet flushing, process water) | Stricter quality and disinfection; **cross-connection control** (never connect to drinking water); signage; reclaimed water pipes are commonly coloured **purple** |
| **Storage lagoons** | Hold effluent for seasonal discharge or irrigation | Level and freeboard; dike inspection |

**[BC-LAW]** In BC, discharges of municipal effluent to **water and to ground**, and the use of **reclaimed water**, are regulated under the **Municipal Wastewater Regulation** (or a site permit under the *Environmental Management Act*). Your facility's registration/permit sets the actual limits and monitoring. *(Confirm details in the regulation and your facility documents — the exam tests the general ideas.)*

### 2.5 Residuals disposal — where the solids go

| Residual | Typical handling |
|---|---|
| **Screenings** | Wash/compact → covered bin → **landfill** |
| **Grit** | Wash/classify (remove organics) → **landfill** |
| **Scum** | To digester, scum concentrator, or disposal per plant practice |
| **Sludge / dewatered cake** | Stabilize (digestion, lime, composting) → **beneficial use as biosolids** (land application, compost, land reclamation — under BC's **Organic Matter Recycling Regulation**) or **landfill** |
| **Backwash water, filtrate, supernatant** | **Returned to the head of the plant** (not discharged) |

### 2.6 Chemical dosing: coagulation/flocculation, nutrient enhancement and pH adjustment *(listed in the 2025 NTK)*

**Coagulation and flocculation — making tiny particles clump**
| Step | What happens | Chemicals | Mixing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coagulation** | Very fine particles carry small electrical charges that make them **repel** each other. A **coagulant** neutralizes those charges so they can stick together | **Alum** (aluminum sulphate), **ferric chloride**, ferric sulphate, PACl (polyaluminum chloride) | **Rapid (flash) mix** — seconds |
| **Flocculation** | The destabilized particles collide and grow into larger, heavier **flocs** that settle or filter out | **Flocculant** = **polymer** (long-chain molecule that bridges particles) | **Slow, gentle mix** — minutes (fast mixing breaks flocs) |

- Uses: chemically enhanced primary treatment, **chemical phosphorus removal** (Module 7), helping tertiary filters, thickening/dewatering (polymer — Module 9).
- **The right dose is found by a jar test**: several beakers of the same water dosed at different rates, mixed fast then slow, then settled — pick the dose that gives the clearest water with the least chemical.
- **Overdosing** wastes chemical, can **re-stabilize** particles (poorer results), and lowers pH. **Underdosing** = pin-sized flocs that don't settle.
- Metal coagulants **consume alkalinity and lower pH**.

**Nutrient enhancement (nutrient addition)**
Some industrial wastes (e.g., food processing) are rich in BOD but **short of nitrogen or phosphorus**. Bugs need about **BOD:N:P = 100:5:1** — if N or P is missing, treatment and settling suffer (bulking). Operators may **add nutrients** (e.g., urea or ammonium compounds for N; phosphoric acid or phosphate for P) to restore the balance.

**pH adjustment**
| Need | Chemicals | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Raise pH / add alkalinity** (nitrification ate the alkalinity, acidic waste) | **Lime**, **caustic soda** (sodium hydroxide), **sodium bicarbonate**, magnesium hydroxide, soda ash | Caustic is very corrosive; lime is dusty; bicarbonate is the safest/gentlest |
| **Lower pH** (alkaline industrial waste) | Sulphuric acid, carbon dioxide | Add acid to water; corrosive |

**Chemical dosing equipment:** storage tanks with **secondary containment**, **metering pumps** (diaphragm, peristaltic, progressive cavity — Module 10), day tanks, **calibration columns** (to check the pump's actual feed rate), injection quills/diffusers, flow-pacing controls (Module 13). Check feed rates daily, look for leaks and airlocks, and keep incompatible chemicals apart (Module 2).

## 3. Operator-level understanding

- **Filters protect the effluent but depend on the clarifier.** If filters are backwashing constantly, look **upstream** (bulking, solids carryover).
- **EQ basins must not go septic** — keep them mixed/aerated and empty them after events.
- **Odour control is also safety** — H₂S is toxic, and covered channels/wet wells collect it.
- **Rapid infiltration basins need rest periods** — loading one basin continuously clogs it.
- **Reclaimed water = drinking-water-style cross-connection control.**

## 4. MUST MEMORIZE

1. Tertiary filters remove the fine solids the clarifier missed → lower TSS, turbidity, P, BOD; help UV.
2. Rising **headloss** → time to **backwash**; backwash water returns to the plant.
3. Short filter runs = too many solids coming from upstream.
4. **Flow equalization** stores peaks and releases flow evenly; **mix/aerate** to prevent septic conditions; located after screening/grit.
5. Main odour = **H₂S** from **septic** conditions; it is toxic and corrosive.
6. Odour control: covers + **biofilters** (keep moist), **chemical scrubbers** (caustic/hypochlorite), **activated carbon**, chemical addition (iron salts, nitrate, peroxide), pre-aeration.
7. Effluent disposal: **surface outfall/diffuser**, **rapid infiltration basins (alternate wet/dry, scarify)**, exfiltration fields, irrigation, **reclaimed water** (purple pipe, cross-connection control).
8. Screenings & grit → **landfill**; sludge → **biosolids** (OMRR) or landfill; liquid side-streams → **back to the plant**.

9. **Coagulation** (alum/ferric, **rapid mix**) neutralizes particle charges; **flocculation** (polymer, **slow mix**) grows flocs. Dose by **jar test**.
10. **Nutrient enhancement** = add N or P when BOD:N:P is out of balance (≈100:5:1).
11. **pH adjustment:** raise with lime, caustic, sodium bicarbonate; metal coagulants and nitrification **lower** pH.
12. Check metering pump rates with a **calibration column**.

## 6. Common exam traps

| Trap | Truth |
|---|---|
| "Backwash water is discharged with the effluent" | It's **returned to the plant**. |
| "Fix short filter runs by backwashing harder" | Usually the cause is **upstream solids**. |
| "Keep a biofilter dry" | Biofilters must stay **moist** (bacteria need water). |
| "Load a rapid infiltration basin continuously" | **Alternate** loading and resting. |
| "Reclaimed water can be connected to the drinking water system if it's very clean" | **Never** — cross-connection control applies. |
| "Equalization basins should be quiet so solids settle" | They're usually **mixed/aerated** to prevent septic conditions. |

## 8. Answers & explanations

| Q | Ans | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A** | Headloss (or turbidity breakthrough or time) triggers backwash. |
| 2 | **B** | Solids carryover loads the filters quickly → short runs. Fix the clarifier problem. |
| 3 | **B** | Backwash water contains captured solids — it's treated again. |
| 4 | **B** | Equalization smooths flow and load. |
| 5 | **B** | Mixing/aeration prevents settling and septicity. |
| 6 | **A** | H₂S from anaerobic/septic wastewater. |
| 7 | **A** | Bacteria in the media need moisture. |
| 8 | **B** | Resting and scarifying restores infiltration. |
| 9 | **B** | Cross-connection control; purple pipe is the common identifier. |
| 10 | **B** | Landfill (per local rules). |
| 11 | **A** | Caustic and/or hypochlorite absorb and oxidize odorous gases. |
| 12 | **B** | Particles shield organisms from UV; filtration improves UV performance. |
| 13 | **B** | Flocs need gentle mixing; fast mixing tears them apart. |
| 14 | **D** | A jar test compares doses side by side. |
| 15 | **C** | Needed N ≈ 40 mg/L and P ≈ 8 mg/L for 800 mg/L BOD (100:5:1). |
| 16 | **B** | Bicarbonate (or lime/caustic) adds alkalinity; alum, ferric and acid lower pH. |
