**NTK (Need-to-Know Criteria) area:** **Security, Safety and Administrative Procedures (≈13%)** — shared with Module 2 (Safety) and the security part of Module 13.

**Important note on labels:**
- **[BC-LAW]** / **[FEDERAL-LAW]** = an actual statute or regulation (named so you can look it up).
- **[GENERAL]** = good operator practice, not a specific law.
- The WPI (Water Professionals International) exam is used across Canada and the US, so it tests **general operator responsibilities**, not BC section numbers. Know the **ideas**; use the BC names so you understand your real job.
- **Nothing here is Ontario- or US-specific.** If a detail matters for your job, confirm it in the current regulation or your facility's permit/authorization.

## 2. Plain-English teaching

### 2.1 Operator responsibilities — who you work for

As a certified operator, you are responsible to:
| To | Means |
|---|---|
| **The public** | Protect public health (disinfection, preventing sewage backups and spills) |
| **The environment** | Meet discharge limits; prevent and report spills; protect receiving waters |
| **Your employer** | Operate efficiently and safely; maintain equipment; follow SOPs and policies |
| **Co-workers** | Work safely; communicate hazards; never put others at risk |
| **Regulators** | Follow the permit/authorization; keep honest records; report as required |
| **Your profession** | Stay certified, keep learning, work within your certification level, act ethically |

### 2.2 Records — "If it isn't written down, it didn't happen"

**Why records matter:**
- **Legal proof** of compliance (permit/authorization, regulation)
- **Process control** — trends show problems early
- **Maintenance planning** — equipment history, warranty, budgeting
- **Safety** — proof of training, lockouts, confined-space permits, inspections
- **Shift communication** and **investigations** after incidents

**Types of records operators keep [GENERAL]:**
| Record | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Operating logs / daily logbook** | Rounds observations, flows, setpoint changes, equipment started/stopped, unusual events, weather |
| **Lab records** | Bench sheets, results, QC (blanks, duplicates, standards), calibrations, chain-of-custody forms |
| **Flow and process data** | Flow totalizer readings, SCADA (Supervisory Control And Data Acquisition) trends, chemical use |
| **Maintenance records** | **Work orders**, PM checklists, equipment history, lubrication logs, run hours, parts used |
| **Calibration records** | Instruments, gas detectors (bump tests and calibration), meters |
| **Safety records** | Training, confined-space entry permits, lockout records, inspections, **incident/near-miss reports**, first-aid records |
| **Chemical records** | Inventory, deliveries, SDSs, usage |
| **Regulatory reports** | Monthly/annual reports, non-compliance and spill/bypass notifications |
| **Complaints** | Odour/noise/backup complaints and responses |

**Good record-keeping rules [GENERAL]:**
- Record **at the time** (or as soon as possible) — not from memory days later.
- **Accurate, complete, legible**, in **permanent ink** (or secure electronic system).
- **Date, time, units and your name/initials** on every entry.
- **Never erase or use correction fluid.** Draw **one line** through a mistake, write the correction, initial and date it.
- **Never falsify, "adjust" or invent data.** Falsifying records is **illegal** and can end your career (and is a serious offence under environmental law).
- Record **abnormal** events and what you did — not just numbers.
- Keep records for the **retention period** required by your permit/regulation and employer.

### 2.3 Reporting

| Type | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Routine reports** | Monthly/quarterly/annual, per permit/regulation | Flows, effluent quality, exceedances, operational summary |
| **Non-compliance** | When an effluent limit or permit condition isn't met | Report as the permit/regulation requires — usually **promptly** |
| **Spills, overflows, bypasses** | Any unauthorized release of wastewater or chemicals | **Immediate** notification is commonly required (see BC spill reporting below) |
| **Workplace injuries & incidents** | Injury, dangerous occurrence | Report to supervisor immediately; employer reports to WorkSafeBC as required |
| **Near misses** | Nobody was hurt but could have been | Report — they prevent future injuries |
| **Equipment failures** | Failures affecting treatment | Supervisor; may trigger regulatory notification |

**The exam's rule of thumb:** when in doubt, **notify your supervisor** and **follow the reporting procedure**. Never hide a problem.

### 2.4 SOPs, O&M manuals, and emergency plans

| Document | What it is |
|---|---|
| **SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)** | Written **step-by-step** instructions for a task (e.g., "Changing a chlorine cylinder", "Starting blower #2", "Collecting the 24-h composite"). Ensures everyone does it the **same, safe, correct way** |
| **O&M (Operations & Maintenance) manual** | Describes the plant, design criteria, each process, operating ranges, equipment, maintenance requirements, troubleshooting |
| **Manufacturers' manuals** | Equipment-specific operation and maintenance |
| **Emergency Response Plan (ERP)** | What to do in emergencies: power failure, chlorine leak, spill/overflow, fire, flood, toxic discharge, injury, security breach — **contact lists**, roles, procedures |
| **Operating plan** | Under the BC Municipal Wastewater Regulation, a facility has an operating plan that includes, among other things, operator qualifications (see 2.7) |

**Operators should:** follow SOPs; suggest improvements when a procedure is unsafe or outdated; **review the ERP** and practise drills; know where manuals are kept.

### 2.5 Preventive maintenance records & work orders [GENERAL]

- A **work order** describes the job, equipment, who did it, parts, time, and findings.
- **PM schedules** list tasks by frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, annual, run-hours).
- **Equipment history records** show repairs and trends (e.g., the same pump failing every 3 months = a root cause to find).
- Many plants use a **CMMS** (Computerized Maintenance Management System).

### 2.6 Communication [GENERAL]

- **Shift handover:** what's running, what's out of service, locked-out equipment, process changes, alarms, pending lab results, safety concerns — **written and verbal**.
- **Supervisors:** report problems early; don't wait.
- **Public:** be courteous; for complaints, record details and pass them on; **media questions go to the designated spokesperson**.
- **Regulators:** honest, timely, through the proper channels.
- **Contractors/visitors:** site safety orientation, sign-in, hazards, permits.

### 2.7 The BC regulatory framework — who regulates what

   ├──────────────────┤    └──────────────────┘    └──────────────────┘   └──────────────────┘

| Body / law | What it covers | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Environmental Management Act (EMA)** (BC) | Main BC environmental law; prohibits unauthorized waste discharge; authorizes regulations and permits | [BC-LAW] |
| **Municipal Wastewater Regulation (MWR), B.C. Reg. 87/2012** | Rules for municipal wastewater discharges: registration, discharge standards, monitoring, reporting, operating plans. **Section 47:** a discharger must not discharge unless the facility is operated and maintained by persons with the qualifications in the operating plan **who are certified under the EOCP** | [BC-LAW] |
| **Permits / approvals / authorizations under EMA** | Some facilities operate under site-specific permits instead of (or in addition to) MWR registration — your facility's document sets its limits | [BC-LAW] |
| **Organic Matter Recycling Regulation (OMRR)** | Production and land application of **biosolids** and compost | [BC-LAW] |
| **Spill Reporting Regulation** (under EMA) | Requires reporting of spills of specified substances (which can include wastewater) to the provincial emergency reporting line | [BC-LAW — confirm the current reporting number and thresholds with your employer; BC's 24-h emergency line has been **1-800-663-3456**] |
| **Wastewater Systems Effluent Regulations (WSER), SOR/2012-139** (federal, under the *Fisheries Act*) | National minimum effluent standards for systems that collect **≥ 100 m³/day** — e.g., CBOD (Carbonaceous Biochemical Oxygen Demand) **≤ 25 mg/L (milligrams per litre)**, suspended solids **≤ 25 mg/L** (averages), **total residual chlorine ≤ 0.02 mg/L**, un-ionized ammonia **≤ 1.25 mg/L** (as N at 15 °C), plus monitoring, record-keeping and reporting | [FEDERAL-LAW — confirm details] |
| **Workers Compensation Act + OHS (Occupational Health and Safety) Regulation (WorkSafeBC)** | Worker health and safety: confined space (Part 9), lockout (Part 10), WHMIS (Part 5), PPE (Part 8), fall protection (Part 11), working alone, right to refuse | [BC-LAW] |
| **WHMIS 2015** (federal *Hazardous Products Act* + WorkSafeBC) | Chemical labels, SDSs, training | [FEDERAL/BC-LAW] |
| **Transportation of Dangerous Goods (TDG)** | Shipping hazardous materials (chlorine, samples sometimes) — recognize | [FEDERAL-LAW] |
| **Local sewer-use bylaws** | What businesses can discharge to the sewer (limits on FOG (Fats, Oils and Grease), metals, pH, etc.); enforcement against illegal discharges | [Local] |
| **Public Health Act — Sewerage System Regulation** | Applies to **on-site** (septic) systems, not municipal treatment plants — **don't confuse them** | [BC-LAW] |

### 2.8 EOCP certification basics [OFFICIAL — EOCP]

- **EOCP** (Environmental Operators Certification Program) is the certification body for water and wastewater operators in **BC and Yukon**.
- EOCP **classifies facilities** (Level I–IV, by complexity). The level of the operator responsible for a facility should match its classification.
- **Operator levels:** OIT (optional), Level I, II, III, IV. Level I requires **12 months (1,800 h)** of hands-on experience + high school (or equivalent), and passing the exam (70%).
- **Certificates must be renewed**, and operators must earn **continuing education (CEUs)** to stay certified — check the **current EOCP Program Guide** for the exact requirements and fees.
- Operators must **work within their certification level** and are expected to act **ethically** (honest data, safe practice, competence).

### 2.9 Professional responsibilities & ethics [GENERAL]

- **Honesty:** never falsify data or records; report mistakes.
- **Competence:** work within your knowledge and certification; ask when unsure.
- **Safety:** never take shortcuts that put people at risk; use your right to refuse unsafe work.
- **Continuing education:** keep learning (CEUs (Continuing Education Units), courses, conferences, BCWWA (BC Water & Waste Association) training).
- **Confidentiality:** follow employer policies on information, security and media.
- **Public trust:** you protect public health — take it seriously.

### 2.10 Vulnerability assessments, customer complaints and equipment selection *(listed in the 2025 NTK)*

**Vulnerability assessment** — a structured review of **what could go wrong** and **how badly**, so the facility can protect itself:
- Threats: natural (flood, wildfire, earthquake, extreme cold), accidental (chemical spill, power failure, equipment failure), and deliberate (vandalism, theft, intrusion, **cyber attack on SCADA**).
- For each critical asset (influent pumps, disinfection, power supply, SCADA, chemical storage): how likely is the threat, what would the consequence be, and what protects it now?
- Results feed the **Emergency Response Plan**, security upgrades (fences, locks, lighting, alarms, cameras, cybersecurity), backup equipment and staff training.
- Operators help by reporting weak spots, doing **routine security checks** (locks, fences, signs of tampering), and keeping the ERP and contact lists current.

**Customer service requests and complaints** (odour, noise, sewer backup, trucks, appearance):
1. Be **courteous**; listen and take the person seriously.
2. **Record** the details — name/contact, date, time, location, what they noticed, weather/wind.
3. **Check** plant operations at that time (e.g., sludge hauling, a tank out of service).
4. **Respond** per procedure; refer media or legal questions to the designated person.
5. **Follow up** and keep records — complaint logs help find patterns and may be required by the permit.
A sewer **backup into a home** is urgent — follow the emergency procedure and notify the supervisor immediately.

**Assisting in equipment selection:** operators know what fails, what's hard to maintain, and what's unsafe. When new equipment is chosen, operators contribute: **reliability and maintenance history**, **parts availability**, **ease and safety of maintenance** (lifting points, access, lockout points), **standardizing** with existing equipment, energy use, and suitability for the job (e.g., solids-handling pumps for raw sludge).

## 3. Operator-level understanding

- **Your logbook is a legal document.** Write clearly, in ink, at the time; correct with a single line, initialled.
- **A spill or bypass is never "too small to report"** without checking the procedure — know your reporting thresholds and numbers before you need them.
- **Shift handover** prevents accidents — especially for **locked-out** equipment and **process changes**.
- **SOPs protect you** — if you follow the SOP and something goes wrong, you acted correctly. If the SOP is wrong, report it and get it fixed.
- **Know your facility's permit/registration limits** and monitoring requirements — they are your "scorecard".

## 4. MUST MEMORIZE

1. Records must be **accurate, legible, timely, permanent, signed/initialled, dated**; correct with **one line + initials**; **never falsify**.
2. **Report:** spills, overflows, bypasses, permit exceedances, injuries, near misses — **promptly, per procedure**.
3. **SOP** = step-by-step instructions; **O&M manual** = how the plant works and is maintained; **ERP** = emergency procedures and contacts.
4. **Preventive maintenance** uses schedules and **work orders**; equipment history reveals repeat failures.
5. **BC:** **Environmental Management Act → Municipal Wastewater Regulation** (discharges; **s.47 requires EOCP-certified operators**), **OMRR** (biosolids), **Spill Reporting Regulation**.
6. **Federal:** **Fisheries Act → Wastewater Systems Effluent Regulations** (e.g., CBOD 25, SS 25, TRC (Total Residual Chlorine) 0.02 mg/L).
7. **WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation** = worker safety (confined space Part 9, lockout Part 10).
8. **EOCP** certifies operators and classifies facilities in **BC & Yukon**; Level I = **1,800 h**, **70%** pass; renew with **CEUs**.
9. **Sewer-use bylaws** = local control of industrial/commercial discharges.
10. On-site septic systems fall under **public health** rules — a different system from municipal plants.

11. **Vulnerability assessment** = identify threats (natural, accidental, deliberate/cyber) to critical assets → feeds ERP and security upgrades.
12. **Complaints:** courteous → record details → check operations → respond per procedure → follow up and log.

## 6. Common exam traps

| Trap | Truth |
|---|---|
| "Use correction fluid to fix a logbook mistake" | **Single line, initial, date.** |
| "Small spills don't need reporting" | Follow the reporting procedure — many spills must be reported. |
| "Estimate missing lab data from past results" | **Never** fabricate data; record it as missing and why. |
| "Near misses aren't worth reporting" | They **must** be reported — they prevent injuries. |
| "Talk to the media about an incident if asked" | Refer to the **designated spokesperson**. |
| "WorkSafeBC sets effluent limits" | WorkSafeBC = **worker safety**. Effluent = **ENV/MWR/permits** and **federal WSER**. |
