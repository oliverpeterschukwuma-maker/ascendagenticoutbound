# Official Exam Formula Sheet — Summary & What Changed

**Source:** *Formula/Conversion Table — Wastewater Treatment, Collection, Industrial Waste & Wastewater Laboratory Exams* (Association of Boards of Certification, © 2017; file name "ABC-2018-Wastewater-Formula-Sheet"; hosted by EOCP). EOCP lists the "ABC Canadian Formula/Conversion Table for Wastewater Treatment, Industrial, Collection and Laboratory Exams" among documents **provided at all exam sessions**. The PDF itself is copyrighted and not stored here — keep your own printed copy to practise with.

**8 pages:** pages 1–6 formulas (alphabetical), page 7 abbreviations + conversion factors, page 8 "pie wheels".

## What the course changed after checking the sheet
1. **9.81 → 9.8** everywhere (1 m of water = 9.8 kPa; power kW = 9.8 × m³/s × m). All affected answers were recalculated.
2. **New lesson [17F](../math/17f-more-official-formulas.md)** for formulas on the sheet that weren't taught: alkalinity, CFU/100 mL, composite portion, cycle time, RAS solids balance, recirculation ratio, organic loading (TF/RBC), population equivalent, slope, specific gravity, SDI, volatile solids reduction, total solids %, blending (three normal), backwash rate/rise, power from L/s, motor & wire-to-water efficiency, AC watts, % stroke, US feed-pump mL/min.
3. **Metric SOR and WOR on the sheet use litres:** Lpd/m² and Lpd/m (1 m³/m²·d = 1,000 Lpd/m²). Warnings added in 17B.
4. **SRT/MCRT on the sheet includes clarifier solids** — note added in 17D.
5. **Pie wheels** explained (same idea as the triangle method) in 17A and on the formula-sheet page.
6. The [formula & conversion sheet](../math/formula-and-conversion-sheet.md) now lists every formula in the official order with the lesson that teaches it, plus the official conversion factors.

## Not on the official sheet — you must know these yourself
- **Dose = Demand + Residual** (chlorine)
- CT = concentration × time
- Thickening volume (V₂ = V₁ × %₁ ÷ %₂) and kg/d of dry sludge = m³/d × % × 10
- Basic metric facts: 1 m³ = 1,000 L; 1 ML = 1,000 m³; 1 day = 1,440 min = 86,400 s; 1 L/s = 86.4 m³/d
