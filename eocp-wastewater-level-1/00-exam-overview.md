## 1. What exam you are taking

**[OFFICIAL]** EOCP (the Environmental Operators Certification Program, BC's operator certification body) uses the **WPI (Water Professionals International, formerly ABC (Association of Boards of Certification)) standardized Level I–IV exams**. EOCP introduced the **new standardized WPI exams in July 2025**. These replaced older exam versions — so old "EOCP exam blueprints" or old practice exams may not match what you will see.

**[OFFICIAL]** EOCP's exam-preparation guidance tells candidates to:
1. Study the **Need-to-Know Criteria (NTK)** for their exam (for you: *WPI Wastewater Treatment Operator Class I*).
2. Review **sample exam questions**.
3. Learn the **computerized exam format**.
4. Use the **EOCP/WPI math and formula resources** — in particular:
   - *Math for Operators — Wastewater Level I & II* (EOCP)
   - *Guide to the EOCP and ABC Formulas* (G. Faris, 2022)
   - The **Formula/Conversion Table** supplied with the exam.

**[OFFICIAL]** WPI publishes a Need-to-Know document for Wastewater Treatment Class I (2025 version), which lists the **content areas** and the **job tasks** tested, plus the reference books the questions are drawn from. **✅ Received and incorporated** (see the table in section 2).

**[OFFICIAL]** WPI exams are designed for use in both Canada and the US, so **calculation questions are presented in both US customary and metric units**. You must be comfortable with both.

**[OFFICIAL]** The EOCP math guides state that **π (pi) = 3.14** is the value used in all calculations on EOCP exams. Use 3.14 (or 0.785 for D² area formulas) — do not use your calculator's π button, or your answer may not match the choices exactly.

### Who can write Level I (EOCP eligibility) [OFFICIAL — EOCP "How to Become an Operator"]
- **Level I:** 12 months (**1,800 hours**) of hands-on operating experience **at a Class I or higher facility** — this is the requirement to be **eligible to write** the exam. Hours are verified with the **Supervisor Verification Form** (required for every exam application).
- **Employment:** you must be **employed, or have an employment offer letter, with an employer in BC or Yukon**.
- **Education:** High school — diploma/transcript, BC Adult Graduation Diploma, Red Seal certificate, post-secondary diploma requiring high school, or **CAEC** (replaced the GED in 2024). No extra formal water/wastewater education is required.
- **Location:** You must work or live in **BC or Yukon**.
- **Related experience:** At the EOCP Board's discretion, related work (maintenance, labs, other water/pollution-control jobs, allied trades such as plumber or millwright) can count for **up to half** of the Level I experience.
- **OIT (Operator in Training):** **Optional.** Needs 3 months (500 hours) of hands-on experience **or** 90 hours (9.0 CEUs (Continuing Education Units)) of an approved, directly applicable course. Useful if you're starting out before you have 1,800 hours.

> **Planning tip for writing "next year":** count your hours now. If you won't have 1,800 hours by the exam date, ask EOCP whether OIT or partial related-experience credit fits your situation.

## 2. How the WPI Need-to-Know is organized (and how this course maps to it)

**[OFFICIAL]** The 2025 WPI Wastewater Treatment Class I Need-to-Know groups the job tasks into **four content areas**. The weights below come from the WPI document as quoted in search results and match an independent prep publisher's summary. Confirm them when you open the PDF.

| WPI content area | Share of exam (~100 scored Qs) | What it covers | Where it's taught |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Equipment Evaluation, Maintenance and/or Operation** | **≈ 39%** ← biggest! | Pumps, valves, motors, blowers, drives, generators/auxiliary power, instruments, online analyzers, SCADA/telemetry, preventive & corrective maintenance | Modules 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 (+ equipment parts of 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9) |
| **Treatment Process Evaluation and Adjustment** | **≈ 38%** | Preliminary, primary, biological, clarification, nutrients, disinfection, solids handling, effluent disposal; recognizing and fixing problems | Modules 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 16 |
| **Security, Safety and Administrative Procedures** | **≈ 13%** | Safety programs, hazards, confined space, lockout, chemicals, security, records, reporting, regulations | Modules 2, 18 |
| **Laboratory Analysis** | **≈ 10%** | Sampling, sample handling, lab tests and what results mean | Module 15 |

**What this means for you:**
- **Equipment is the largest area.** Many people study only the treatment process and lose easy marks on pumps, valves, motors and maintenance. Don't skip Modules 10–14.
- **Math is only about 10% of the exam** — **10 calculation questions**: **9 in Treatment Process** (detention time, loading, dosage, SVI (Sludge Volume Index), F/M (Food-to-Microorganism ratio) and similar) and **1 in Laboratory**. Equipment and Safety/Admin have **no** calculation questions. Calculations show **US units first, metric in brackets**, and each is solvable in either system.
- The NTK sorts questions by thinking level: **recall** (remember a fact), **application** (use knowledge in a situation) and **calculation**. Most questions are application — so don't just memorize; understand *why*.

*(CSUS = California State University, Sacramento — Office of Water Programs. WEF = Water Environment Federation. The full WPI reference list is at gowpi.org → ABC Testing → Exam References.)*

**What this means for you:**
- **60% of questions are "application"** — they describe a situation and ask you to choose, compare, explain or calculate. Understanding *why* matters more than memorizing.
- **Calculation questions are only ~10%**, and almost all of them are in treatment process (detention time, loading, dosage, SVI, F/M and similar) — plus one lab calculation.
- **Calculation questions give US units first, then metric in brackets** — e.g., "12 ft (4 m)". You can solve with **either** set; pick the one you're most comfortable with (for most Canadians, metric).
- **[OFFICIAL — EOCP 2022 math guide]** The metric values in brackets are **rounded to one decimal**, so metric and US answers can differ slightly. Example from the guide: *"A clarifier is 100 feet (30.5 m) in diameter and 15 feet (4.8 m) deep"* → *117,750 cubic feet (3,505.2 cubic metres)*. (15 ft is really 4.57 m — so even official brackets can be a little off.) **Stay in one unit system for the whole question** and pick the closest answer.
- **Pre-test questions** (up to 10) are unmarked and mixed in — treat every question seriously.
- **Watch for extra information.** WPI's own sample calculation gives a tank's **diameter** and **water depth** and asks for the pressure at the bottom — the diameter is a distractor, because pressure depends only on depth (Module 12).
- WPI's own sample recall question gives **30 minutes** as the typical chlorine contact time, and its sample application question tests **why activated sludge must be wasted** (to prevent excessive solids build-up).

See [sources/NTK-2025-summary.md](sources/NTK-2025-summary.md) for every job task in the NTK and the module that covers it.

### Level I vs higher levels — what "Level I depth" means
Level I questions mostly test whether you can:
- **Recognize** equipment and processes and state their purpose.
- **Know** normal operating ranges and what "abnormal" looks like.
- **Follow** safe procedures and SOPs (Standard Operating Procedures).
- **Do** basic calculations with the formula sheet (area, volume, flow, detention time, loading, dose, % removal, basic pump/pressure).
- **Choose** the correct first response to a common problem.

Level I does **not** expect you to design processes, do advanced kinetics, or manage complex BNR (Biological Nutrient Removal) optimization. This course keeps Level II–IV material out unless you need it to understand a Level I idea (and flags it when it appears).

## 3. How WPI questions are built — and how to beat them

WPI questions are written from **job tasks**, so most questions are really asking: *"What does a competent operator know or do here?"*

### The five question styles you will see
| Style | Example stem | How to attack it |
|---|---|---|
| **Recall** | "What is the primary purpose of a grit chamber?" | Know the definition cold (flashcards) |
| **Relationship** | "If the F/M (Food-to-Microorganism ratio) ratio increases, the sludge age will most likely…" | Know which way things move together (see "relationships" tables in each module) |
| **Scenario / troubleshooting** | "An operator observes billowing white foam on the aeration tank. The most likely cause is…" | Symptom → most likely cause for *that* symptom (Module 16) |
| **Best action** | "What should the operator do FIRST?" | Safety first → verify the reading → least drastic effective action → notify/record |
| **Calculation** | "A clarifier 18 m in diameter… calculate the surface overflow rate." | Formula sheet + units + sanity check (Module 17) |

### How hard is it? [OFFICIAL — EOCP *Operator Digest*]
| Year | Wastewater Treatment **Level I** pass rate |
|---|---|
| 2018 | **64%** |
| 2020 | **68%** |

WWT I has one of the **lowest Level I pass rates** (Water Treatment I was 91–95%). EOCP says operators struggle most with **math**, and that taking only a prep course isn't enough — **lots of practice questions** is what passing operators did. One operator who passed said there was **ample time**: they finished, reviewed everything and left after **2.5 hours**.

### Universal test-taking rules
1. **Read the last line first.** Know what is being asked (units? first action? most likely cause?).
2. **Watch qualifier words:** *most likely, first, best, NOT, EXCEPT, least, primary.* "NOT/EXCEPT" questions flip the logic — mark them mentally.
3. **Safety beats everything.** If one answer protects life and the others are about the process, the safety answer is usually right for "first" questions.
4. **Verify before you adjust.** When an instrument reads something strange, the first step is usually to *confirm the reading* (check the instrument, take a manual sample) — not to make a big process change.
5. **Make one change at a time, and make small changes.** Large sudden changes are rarely the "best" operator answer.
6. **For math: estimate first.** If a detention time comes out as 400 hours or a flow velocity of 90 m/s, you made a unit error.
7. **Never leave a question blank.** [OFFICIAL] The ABC candidate instructions say **unanswered questions are counted wrong** — so a guess can only help.
8. **Flag and move on.** [OFFICIAL] The ABC exam lets you **mark questions for review, jump to any question, and change answers** until you finish. Do easy questions first, return to long math at the end.
9. **Read every word.** EOCP's example: *"two clarifiers 30 m wide…"* — speed-readers miss "two" and get half the volume.
10. **Trust your first answer.** Change it only when you spot a real mistake.

## 4. The reference hierarchy used in this course

1. **Current EOCP/WPI Need-to-Know Criteria** — defines what can be tested.
2. **Current EOCP exam-preparation page and documents** — format, process, sample questions.
3. **EOCP/WPI formula & conversion material** — defines the formulas and constants (π = 3.14).
4. **Echelon Institute WPI-aligned material** — supplemental explanations, process guides, flashcards (independent provider — *not* the authority).
5. **Standard operator references** that WPI lists for treatment exams (e.g., *Operation of Wastewater Treatment Plants* Vol. I & II, California State University Sacramento / Office of Water Programs) — used for typical values and troubleshooting logic.

**Typical values warning [SUPPLEMENTAL]:** Operating ranges (e.g., "DO (Dissolved Oxygen) 1–3 mg/L (milligrams per litre) in aeration", "SVI (Sludge Volume Index) 80–150 mL/g") vary slightly between textbooks. Learn the *ranges and the direction of change*; an exam question will not hinge on 2.0 vs 2.2.

**BC-specific warning:** This course does **not** present Ontario, Alberta, or US rules as BC requirements. BC/federal legislation is identified by name in Module 18 and labelled **[BC-LAW]** or **[FEDERAL-LAW]**.
